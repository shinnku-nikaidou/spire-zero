Slay the Spire 2 is a single-player card game. The player climbs a tower with a deck of cards, fighting, taking new cards and picking up relics along the way, until the last boss is beaten or the player dies on the way up. This chapter covers only what the later chapters need. Every number is read from the game's own code, version v0.113.1.

## A run

A **run** has three acts. Each act is a map, and each point on the map is a room. Each room passed counts as one floor, and the three acts add up to 48 floors. Each act opens with an Ancient, who restores HP to full and gives a gift, and ends with a boss. Beating the act 3 boss wins the run. When HP reaches zero, the run is over.

Rooms come in these kinds:

- **Combat**: monsters, an elite or a boss. A win gives gold and a choice of one card out of three to add to the deck, or none. Elites are harder and also give a relic.
- **Rest site**: heal 30% of max HP or upgrade one card, but only one of the two.
- **Shop**: spend gold on cards, relics and potions, or on removing a card from the deck.
- **Treasure**: one relic.
- **Event**: a few options, each with a cost and a benefit.
- **"?" room**: only on entering does the player learn whether it is an event, a combat, a shop or a treasure.

A relic is a passive effect that lasts the whole run. A potion is a one-use item, and the player can carry at most three.

## Combat

The player and the enemies take turns. The player's turn starts with 3 Energy and a draw of 5 cards. Playing a card costs Energy, and cards left unplayed are discarded at the end of the turn. Cards go from the draw pile to the hand and, once played, to the discard pile. When the draw pile runs out, the discard pile is shuffled back into it.

Above each enemy the game shows its **intent**, which is what it will do next: attack for how much and how many times, or defend, or strengthen itself. So the player always knows how much damage is coming this turn, but not the turn after.

Block stops damage for this turn. HP does not come back by itself: HP lost in this combat is still missing in the next.

The game has six characters, each with its own cards and style of play. This book's examples use the Silent, who wins with Poison, Shivs and discarding. The game also has 10 Ascension levels, each harder than the one before. At Ascension 10, act 3 ends in two bosses in a row.

## What the seed decides and what stays hidden

Every run has a **seed**, a string of 12 characters. The seed and the player's choices decide everything: the shape of the map, who appears in each combat, how the cards are shuffled and what the rewards hold. The same seed with the same choices always gives the same run.

The whole project rests on this. Because of it, a run can be replayed exactly, the AI can try things on a copy before it acts, and two AIs can play the same seeds to see which is stronger.

The seed decides everything, but the player does not see all of it:

| Visible | Hidden |
|---|---|
| The whole map of this act, the type of every point, who the boss is | What a "?" room is before the player enters |
| Each enemy's intent for its next move | Its moves after that |
| Which cards are in the draw pile | The order of the draw pile |
| The HP, Block and status effects of the player and the enemies | Who the next combat is against, what the rewards will be, what the shops sell |

Whether the AI may use the hidden column is the subject of the chapter "Two environments".

## Why it is hard

- **The road is long.** The strongest version of the AI so far, which this book calls the champion, makes about 550 decisions in an average run. A card taken on floor 2 is still in the deck on floor 48.
- **There is only one result.** Hundreds of decisions share one win or loss, so it is hard to say which decision was right.
- **Much is hidden.** Every decision is made without knowing the draw order, the "?" rooms or the next combat.
- **The options are many and keep changing.** In one turn the cards in hand can be played in any order, and each card may also need a target.
- **One death ends it.** HP is a budget to spread over twenty-odd combats, and every rest site is a choice between healing and an upgrade.
