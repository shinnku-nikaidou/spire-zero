This page is the record: what was trained, on what, and what it measured, in the order it happened. The design pages say how things work now ([Macro brain: the value network](/en/arch/macro-brain/network/), [Macro brain: from hazards to value](/en/arch/macro-brain/value/), [Training the combat net](/en/arch/learning/combat-net/), [The loop](/en/arch/learning/loop/)); this one keeps the numbers that led there, including those of methods since removed. Dates are the data's (the ledger's, the reports', the commits'). Unless said otherwise, a *report* is 200 runs on the seeds `promotion-1`, floors are means, ± is one standard error, and a *paired* difference is challenger less champion on the same seeds.

The sources are in git: the ledger `eval/silent/ledger.md`, the gauntlet leaderboard `eval/silent/gauntlet-leaderboard.md`, the reports in `eval/silent/reports/`, each net's training report (`fit`) in `weights/silent/NAME.json`, and the commit messages. The raw runs behind them are outside git ([Reference §3](/en/arch/learning/reference/#sec-3)).

## 2. 2026-10-27: the first leaves and V v0's fit

**The NoSL champion's line** (whole runs, `promotion-1`, NoSL; [Evaluation: champions and baselines §2](/en/arch/evaluation/champions/#sec-2)). With V v0's hand-set weights in the macro brain, the combat brain went from `play` (floor 11.90) to PIMC with `LeafValue` (19.34, act 1 boss reached in 177 runs; it passed its gate against `play` after 14 pairs, +8.21 floors) to PIMC with the combat net c2 (21.88, act 1 boss in 187 runs, act 2 boss in 45; passed against `LeafValue` after 77 pairs, +2.36 floors). After fixes to the simulator it was re-baselined at 22.70 (`432aa0c`), 22.58 (`32a0160`) and 22.58 (`c5ee8de`) ([Evaluation: gates §9](/en/arch/evaluation/gates/#sec-9)). That last report, act 1 boss beaten in 94 of the 190 runs that reached it and 0.39 elites a run, is the champion the V v2 nets were first gated against.

**g0** (NoSL, PIMC 8 × 300 with `LeafValue`, L0 with V v0 exploring at 0.2; commits `1b80389`, `f355299`; train seeds 0–8999): 356 runs, 2 failed. c1 and c2 were trained on its 315 runs; their numbers are in [Training the combat net §5](/en/arch/learning/combat-net/#sec-5).

**V v0's fit** (`learn/fit_value.py`, removed in `9b9d170`): a ridge regression of V v0's 17 features, which every macro decision in the decision logs carries ([Harness: the decision log §7](/en/arch/harness/decision-log/#sec-7)), on the floor its run reached. *Ridge regression* is least squares with a small penalty on the size of the weights, which keeps correlated features from taking extreme weights. How it joined the logs to the runs is what every trainer since does: the run records give the floor each run reached, joined by run id, each log only with the record file its `run` wrote beside it (`--logs` and `--records` list them in the same order); a run id is a hash of the seed alone, so another run of the same seed, by another policy or in an exploration scan, can be in other files; a run id twice in one record file is refused. Each run weighs the same however many decisions it has, and every fifth run (by run id) is held out. The report gave $R^2$ on the held-out runs (the share of the floor's variance the fit explains: 1 at best, below 0 when it does worse than the mean), next to a fit on the floor alone. Refit on g0 and g1, V v0's features reach 0.315, no more than the floor alone (0.313). No fitted weights were promoted.

## 3. n1: V v2's first net

**g1** (NoSL, PIMC with c2 exploring at 0.003, L0 with V v0 exploring at 2; commits `11652b0`, `217af09`, `432aa0c`; train seeds 30000–34999): 508 runs, 3 failed.

**First measurements** of V v2 (`learn/train_value.py`) on g0 and g1, 859 runs and 60,480 macro decisions, one fold. The held-out loss is 1.229 against the baseline's 1.291, and the $R^2$ 0.368 against 0.313. The decisions of a run share its one outcome, so there are about 700 outcomes to learn from, and the net overfits fast:

- from a random head at learning rate 10⁻³ it memorizes the runs within an epoch: held-out loss 1.331 after the first, 4.12 after the sixth;
- started at the baseline it is 1.573 at 10⁻³, 1.235 at 10⁻⁴ and 1.229 at 3 × 10⁻⁵; a weight decay of 0.1 instead of 10⁻⁴ changes nothing.

**Its card sense** (`ranks` over g1's logs) puts the poison cards first in act 0: one more OUTBREAK is worth 0.77 floors, then SNAKEBITE, BUBBLE_BUBBLE, BOUNCING_FLASK and DEADLY_POISON. In act 1, which far fewer runs reach, it is noise: the curses, too rare to have ids of their own, rank near the top at +0.1.

**The second head.** n1 was trained without it. With it, at weight 3, on the same runs and three training seeds, the held-out loss is 1.222 against 1.235 on average (1.208, 1.237 and 1.222 against 1.229, 1.246 and 1.232) and the $R^2$ 0.384 against 0.371, the loss lower and the $R^2$ higher in every seed; at weights 1 and 10 (one seed) 1.224 and 1.210.

**n1's gate**, L0 with n1 against the champion's L0 with V v0's initial weights, the combat the same (c2), failed after 157 of the 200 seeds. On all 200 it reached floor 21.97 against 22.58 (paired −0.61 ± 1.32). It plays otherwise: it skipped 15 of 2,261 card rewards against 906 of 2,081, and fought 0.94 elites a run against 0.39. It beat the act 1 boss more often (100 of the 171 runs that reached it, against 94 of 190) but died more on the way there and in act 2, where far fewer of its training runs went: 22 runs reached the act 2 boss against 45. Taking nearly every card is what the training runs show, not what one card does: runs that won more fights took more cards and went deeper, so a bigger deck reads as a deeper floor. Exploration and more runs give the net the comparisons that tell the two apart.

## 4. Counterfactual pairs, step 0

More runs of the same kind do not untangle the deck from the luck ([§3](#sec-3)); the same state played on with two choices and the same luck does ([The loop §10](/en/arch/learning/loop/#sec-10)). The `afterstates` and `fork` commands were built for V v2's next generation.

- **Replays.** g1's 508 runs replay exactly. g0's 356 reach the same ends on the same surfaces, but most of their observation hashes differ, since the observation has gained fields since. A replay takes about 0.7 s a run.
- **27 forks over g1's runs.** The floors the two branches reached correlate at ρ = 0.66 (0.64 with the share of HP left), so a difference has about a third of the variance two independent runs would have. 20 of the 27 had been drawn on rewards screens, whose choice is mostly the order of taking the rewards, and 17 of those tied; rewards screens are left out by default since, and the surfaces take turns. Most pairs end the act alike (both died at the boss, or both lived); what tells them apart is the HP and the deck at the act's end. A fork takes about 70 s of one process for both branches.

## 5. n2: the pull and the forks

**g2** (NoSL, PIMC with c2 at 0.003, L0 with V v0 at 0.25; commits `5032064`, `f11d11c`; train seeds 40000–47999): 339 runs.

**n2**, on g0–g2 (1,198 runs, 85,089 macro decisions), with the second head:

- **The outcome-only net** repeats n1's pattern. On card rewards its best option is a skip in 0.2% of the held-out decisions, against V v0's 14.4%; its best is V v0's in 56% of the decisions, 35% on card rewards and 26% in shops.
- **The pull** ([§3](#sec-3)). Its temperature comes from the slope of V's values on V v0's within a decision, 0.031: V v0's 0.25 is about 0.008 floors, so the pull runs at 0.01. At that temperature its gradient is about β / 0.01 times the outcome loss's. At β = 1 the held-out outcome loss did not move in three epochs: V copied V v0's ranking. At β = 0.1 it is 1.319 against the floor-only 1.350 ($R^2$ 0.330 against 0.312); V's best is V v0's in 93.5% of the decisions, and it skips 13.1% of the card rewards (V v0: 13.8%). At β = 0.03: 1.318, 89.9% and 15.9%. At β = 0.01: 1.317, 84.4% and 14.1% ($R^2$ 0.335).
- **Forks**: 651 over g1's and g2's runs (about 111 s of one process each). The two branches' floors reached plus HP share left correlate at ρ = 0.62; in 303 pairs both died (all but a few at the act 1 boss), in 226 both lived, in 122 one did. L0's best did better than the other action by 0.24 on the map, 0.10 at rest sites, 0.08 on card rewards and 0.07 in shops, in floors reached within the act plus HP share left: within its act, one choice moves little. On the held-out forks the sign of V's predicted difference matched the played one 0.605 of the time for β = 0.1 and 0.658 for β = 0.03, against V v0's 0.605 and 0.615 on the same forks (54 and 74 of them: these two nets were trained while the forks were still being played); for β = 0.01, on all 114, 0.698 against 0.643.
- **Forks and pull together** (β = 0.1, fork and pair weights 1): the held-out outcome loss is 1.359, above the floor-only 1.350 ($R^2$ 0.273), though the held-out forks' sign accuracy is 0.685 against V v0's 0.643. Why the forks hurt the fit to the self-play outcomes is open; one guess is that the branches, which the greedy champion plays, die at other rates than the exploring self-play. It was not gated.
- **The off-policy check** (inverse propensity scoring) cannot rank these nets: on 4,703 held-out decisions, another draw than V v0's best reached −0.37 ± 1.23 floors against the best, while every net expects about −0.02.

**n2-b0.1's gate**, L0 with it against the champion's, the combat the same (c2), passed after 122 of the 200 seeds at +2.00 floors (LLR 2.95). On all 200 it reached floor 23.96 against 22.58 (paired +1.39 ± 0.65). It beat the act 1 boss in 117 of the 186 runs that reached it, against 94 of 190, and reached the act 2 boss 62 times against 45; it skipped 36% of the card rewards against 44%, and fought 0.44 elites a run against 0.39. It was the NoSL champion's V from 2026-10-28 until n2-b0.03 replaced it the same day.

**n2-b0.03's gate**, against n2-b0.1, passed after 136 of the 200 seeds at +1.78 floors (LLR 2.99). On all 200 it reached floor 26.16 against 23.96 (paired +2.20 ± 0.63). It beat the act 1 boss in 137 of the 190 runs that reached it, against 117 of 186, and reached the act 2 boss 83 times against 62; it skipped 34% of the card rewards against 36%. Its first play, on the build before two fixes, had one failed and two truncated runs: a rest site's smithing that V then declined to use, which went back to the rest site and chose smithing again, and a builder that could not give WATERFALL_GIANT's eruption a value past 99 ([Macro brain: search §1](/en/arch/macro-brain/search/#sec-1), [Simulator: combats §5](/en/arch/simulator/combat/#sec-5)). Played again on the fixed build, the verdict is the same, and 182 of the 200 seeds end as in the first play. It was the NoSL champion's V from 2026-10-28 to 2026-10-30, and the SL champion's from 2026-10-28 (report at `3699fc1`: floor 28.25, act 1 boss reached 192 times, act 2 boss 105, act 3 boss 4, 0.47 elites a run, 194.8 s a run) to 2026-10-29. Its NoSL report (`377e180`): 26.16, 190 / 83 / 3 bosses reached, 0.47 elites, 33.8% card rewards skipped, 159.6 s a run.

**n2-b0.01**'s gate was stopped halfway. Each n2 net has one training seed; the ablations (the pull alone, forks alone, both, against the outcome-only net) would need three each, and were not run before V v2's trainer was removed.

## 9. Plan v6: the elite gauntlet (2026-10-29/30)

The **elite gauntlet** (`run --gauntlet`) is the simplified run plan v6 trained and judged V in: every macro decision is made as usual, by L0 on the node's clone; a plain monster combat is won at its start, with its rewards; elites and bosses are fought by the SL planner, HP and potions carried in and out, and a death ends the run ([Simulator: whole runs §7](/en/arch/simulator/runs/#sec-7)). Why it was chosen:

- bosses and elites killed most runs: the SL champion's runs then died at a boss in 134 of 200, while a plain monster combat cost 3% (act 1) to 18% of max HP on average;
- a gauntlet run takes 45–53 s of a slot at the low budget, a fraction of a whole SL run;
- elites are fought, so an elite costs HP; a first version that won elites at their start was dropped before it ran, since V would learn that more elites are better.

**The budget.** On 515 boss doors at full HP, two seeds at the low budget correlate 0.83, and the low and the medium budget on the same seed 0.94: the cheaper budget ranks doors as well as a second seed of the same one.
