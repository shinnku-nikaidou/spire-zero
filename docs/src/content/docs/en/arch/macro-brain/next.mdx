## 1. What the whole runs show

v6-3-2 wins 23 of 300 whole SL runs. Its 277 losses, and the runs of v6-2 before it, show where the macro brain is weakest.

- **Most losses come between bosses.** About 144 of v6-3-2's 277 losses come between two bosses (10 before the act 1 boss, about 105 in act 2 before its boss, about 29 in act 3 before its boss), and about 133 at a boss. In the elite gauntlet, where V was trained, the monster fights between bosses were free.
- **V hardly weighs HP before a monster fight.** In v6-2's whole runs, 106 of the 299 losses died in a plain monster combat. Offered a monster and a rest site on the map, v6-2 took the monster in 46% of the cases at 40% of max HP or less (24 of 52), and in 50% at 70–90%. The running example has the same choice: at floor 43 with 24 of 66 HP, v6-3-2 values the monster 0.12 floors above the rest site ([Afterstates §2](/en/arch/macro-brain/afterstates/#sec-2)). In the gauntlet that was right: the monster cost nothing.
- **The gauntlet's ranking is not the whole game's.** v6-3-1 beats v6-2 in the gauntlet (+1.73 ± 0.54), wins 13 whole runs to v6-2's 1, and reaches no higher a floor (−0.26 ± 0.70) ([Self-play §5](/en/arch/macro-brain/selfplay/#sec-5)).
- **Labels carry the later draws.** A state's label in a run that explores more is lower, and V is not told the run's ε ([Self-play §2](/en/arch/macro-brain/selfplay/#sec-2)).
- **The encoder sees the map as a bag and meets cards and relics late.** V cannot tell whether a rest site comes after an elite on the same path, and a card–relic synergy must pass through the pooled deck before it meets the relic bag ([The value network §1](/en/arch/macro-brain/network/#sec-1), [§3](/en/arch/macro-brain/network/#sec-3)). The encoders' net reads both differently ([The value network §4](/en/arch/macro-brain/network/#sec-4)).

## 2. Next steps

The next plan (v7) addresses the first three points and the last; the labels stay as they are for now. Its code is built: the whole runs' objective and gate, and the encoders' net. Its first generation of self-play is being played by v6-3-2, and no net of the plan is trained yet.

**Back to whole runs.** V's self-play and its gates play the whole game: plain monster fights are fought by the SL planner too. Self-play keeps the low budget and the even draws at ε 0.3 / 0.15 / 0.05; gates play the champion's planner at medium effort. The elite gauntlet is no longer used to train or judge V.

**P(win), and a gate on wins.** V is trained on whole runs with the labels of [From hazards to value §2](/en/arch/macro-brain/value/#sec-2): a run that died on floor $e$ ended there, a won run survived every floor, a stopped run is labelled up to where it stopped. Its value is then the chance to survive every floor, $P(\text{win}) = S_{63}$ (objective `win`, `v.train --objective win`), read from the same hazard head; the share head goes. The trainer's report adds the held-out Brier score of that chance: the mean squared gap between the predicted chance and the run's outcome, 1 or 0. The second head keeps the next six combats' HP costs, now monster fights included. The gate pairs the challenger's and the champion's runs by seed on 500 seeds (`promotion-1-500`, whose first 300 are `promotion-1-300`) and compares wins, 1 or 0: a challenger passes when the 95% one-sided lower bound of the mean difference is above 0, which at 500 seeds takes about 2–3 percentage points more wins. Reports keep the floors, each act's boss rate, where the runs died (boss, elite, monster, outside combat, per act), the elites per run, and the monster-or-rest choices by HP.

**A build encoder and a route encoder inside the one V.** The single V and its single score stay ([Afterstates §4](/en/arch/macro-brain/afterstates/#sec-4)); only its input side changes, in the encoders' net ([The value network §4](/en/arch/macro-brain/network/#sec-4)). The build encoder lets cards, relics and potions act on each other before they are pooled; the route encoder reads the map's paths from the boss back. The body, the heads, `IRunValue`, `MacroBrain` and `CloneSearch` do not change. `learn/v/encode.py` stays the one specification, `RunNet` builds the same tensors from it, and the net file's test runs check both sides.

**Generations.** Each generation's self-play runs under a deadline. Two nets are trained on that generation's runs alone: a pure V going on from the champion's weights (v7-1 from v6-3-2, whose share head is dropped), and an encoders' net going on from the previous encoders' net (v7-1e from scratch). Both face the champion in the same gate; if both pass, the one with more wins. A net that passes replaces the V of both champion files.

## 3. Planned, not built

These parts of the macro brain's design predate the current plan and are not built.

### L1: rollout check

L0 decides every time; L1 would be an extra check for close calls, with the evaluation-time budget. It would start when the top two L0 values are within their confidence interval, so L0 cannot tell them apart. A **rollout** plays the game forward with fast policies to see where an option leads. For each candidate:

1. Start from its afterstate in sampled run worlds ([Simulator: worlds and the SL clone §8](/en/arch/simulator/worlds/#sec-8)).
2. Play forward for H nodes (by default up to the next boss) with fast policies: in combat the rollout policy or a shallow search, outside combat L0.
3. Pair the candidates with common random numbers: every candidate is played in the same world seeds, so the difference between them is far less noisy than with independent samples.
4. A candidate's value is the mean of V at the horizon, or the terminal outcome if the run ends first.
5. Choose by the paired difference and its confidence interval.

These counterfactual paired rollouts would also be training signal for V at key decisions. In SL, rollouts past the current node still use sampled worlds ([Environments: information §3](/en/arch/environments/information/#sec-3)). On the map, the decision is the next point only, decided again at every step; close calls there are L1's first use.

### NoSL: run worlds

NoSL has no run worlds yet, so its L0 predicts afterstates from the screen ([Search §1](/en/arch/macro-brain/search/#sec-1)). With run worlds, it would apply each option in K sampled worlds and average V over them, and search multi-step screens as SL's clone does, deduplicating states by observation.

### Interface

The planned C# interface. One entry point serves every non-combat surface; Harness routes by surface name ([Harness: routing, budgets and seeds §1](/en/arch/harness/routing/#sec-1)).

```csharp
// Planned. One entry for every non-combat surface; routing by surface name (docs: harness §4).
public interface IMacroBrain
{
    MacroDecisionResult Decide(MacroRequest request, IWorldSource worlds, Budget budget);
}

public sealed record MacroDecisionResult(
    string First,                          // one of legal[]
    IReadOnlyList<string> Plan,            // rest of this screen (shop buys, reward claims), or of the act's route on the map
    IReadOnlyList<Candidate> Candidates,   // value, CI, samples per first action
    IReadOnlyList<Fact> Facts);            // e.g. deck delta, expected HP at boss, P(win) delta
```

`First` is the action to execute now, `Plan` the rest of this screen (on the map, the rest of the act's route), `Candidates` the value, confidence interval and sample count of each first action, and `Facts` the statements used to explain the choice. Out-of-combat card prompts arrive through the same `card_choice` surface as in-combat prompts; `ChoiceContext.Purpose` says what a prompt is for, and Harness routes by whether a combat is running. The macro policy inside rollouts is L0 with a smaller K, never L1.

### Budget

- **Self-play:** a fixed number of branches, so runs are reproducible; L0 always, L1 only on a sampled subset of close calls, whose results become training data.
- **Assist:** L0 answers at once; L1 refines the answer while the player looks at it. The search is *anytime*: it can stop at any moment and still return its best answer so far.
- **Auto:** L0 + L1 under a wall-clock cap.

### Tests

1. Coverage: every macro surface is reached in simulated runs, and the fallback count is 0.
2. Decision panels: a fixed set of (run state, options) cases whose ground truth comes from large paired rollouts; how often L0 agrees, per surface, shows where V is weakest.
3. NoSL counterfactual: the same public record with different hidden state (`RoomSet` tails, odds, streams) gives the same decision.
4. Determinism with a fixed budget.

## 4. Open questions

1. A cut point per run, so that a decision's label carries no later draws: draws before it, V's best after it, training only on the decisions after it, as AlphaGo's value network was trained.
2. How to check P(win)'s calibration once V decides by it: of the states V gives 30%, about 30% should be won ([Learning: reference §12](/en/arch/learning/reference/#sec-12)).
3. How much path planning V learns from the map with the route encoder, against the bag of tokens.
4. The horizon H of L1 (the next boss or the next act), and how strong the rollout's combat policy must be.
5. In NoSL, the shop as one decision or a sequence; in SL the clone searches it as a sequence.
