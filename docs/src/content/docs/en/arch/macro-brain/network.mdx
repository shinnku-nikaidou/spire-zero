V is a neural network that reads a run and predicts how it will end. This section describes it from its inputs to its output: what it reads, how each layer transforms that, and why it is built that way. The code is `Net` in `learn/v/net.py`, and its inputs are specified by `learn/v/encode.py`. What its output means and how it is trained are in the [next section](/en/arch/macro-brain/value/). To see every step with real numbers first, go to [§9](#sec-9).

## 1. The whole net

Seven parts read seven kinds of input, each on its own: the **deck**, the **relics**, the **potions**, the **context**, the **map**, **six numbers** and the **cards the relics keep**. Their outputs are joined into one vector of 1,030 numbers, and an MLP (a *multi-layer perceptron*: linear layers with a non-linearity between them) turns it into one hazard per floor. Those hazards give the value, P(win).

Only the deck has layers of its own, and the cards the relics keep go through the same ones. The bags are plain sums of learned vectors, and everything that relates one part to another is worked out after the join.

One V values every run: all five characters, every ascension, both environments, with one set of weights (`weights/all/`). The character is one of the context tokens and the ascension one of the six numbers.

## 2. What it reads

V reads **what the run is and its raw state, nothing else**. There is no feature written by hand, no relic's effect, no card's cost: what matters in a card, a relic or a deck is the net's to learn from how runs end. A card's numbers come in only where its id cannot say them, through rows of that card's own ([§3](#sec-3)). A price written by hand ("a potion is worth 8 HP") is a strategy written by hand, and a net that reads it copies it, in ways that runs cannot correct.

Everything comes from the run view that Harness logs at every decision ([Harness: the decision log §7](/en/arch/harness/decision-log/#sec-7)), by `encode.tokens` and `encode.state`:

- **Each card of the deck:** its id, its upgrade level (higher levels read as 3) and its modifier tokens: its enchantment and affliction, if any, as `enchant:ID` and `affliction:ID`, its keywords as `keyword:K`, and what Tinker Time made of a Mad Science, as `tinker:TYPE` and `rider:RIDER`. And three numbers, which a run can grow on a card: its enchantment's and affliction's amounts (Goopy's grows with each play), its damage (The Scythe's) and its block (Genetic Algorithm's), Osty's damage for an Osty attack.
- **Each relic:** its id, with `:used` added once it is used up, and what the combat net reads of it ([The combat net §2](/en/arch/combat-brain/network/#sec-2)): its counter / 10 (0 when none shows, capped at ±10), whether a counter shows, whether it is melted, and its status, the game's when it is not the normal one, else `Spent` when its once-a-combat or once-a-turn effect is used, else `Normal`. And the cards it keeps, by id: Pael's Tooth's.
- **Each potion held:** its id. Empty slots are not tokens; the number of slots is one of the six numbers.
- **The context:** `character:ID`, `act:ID`, `boss:ID`, a second `boss:ID` when the act has two, and `ancient:ID` when it has an ancient.
- **The map:** every point a path still leads to, as its type and floors ahead ([§4](#sec-4)).
- **Six numbers:** the floor, HP, max HP, gold, the ascension and the potion slots.

**What it does not read.** A card's cost and its other numbers, the numbers printed on a relic, and anything of a kept card but its id. Two runs that differ only there are one input.

**Token tables.** The net file holds one table of ids per kind of token. Each lists every id of the game build, sorted, after entry 0, one shared *unknown* id, which reads any other id, such as one newer than the file (`learn/game_ids.py`, from `learn/game_ids.json`, which the simulator's `tables` command writes). So the tables do not depend on the training data. `modifiers` lists the enchantments, afflictions and keywords, Tinker Time's card types and riders, and `spent`, which V is never given; the context's lists the game's acts, ancients, bosses and characters; the map's lists every type of point at 0 to 20 floors ahead, each with and without `:quest`; `relic_status` lists the game's relic statuses and `Spent`, as the combat net's does. A kept card reads the `cards` table.

**The numbers** are standardized, $(x - \mu)/\sigma$, by the mean and standard deviation of the training samples, each sample weighted by one over its run's samples. A standard deviation under $10^{-9}$ reads as 1. The net file keeps $\mu$ and $\sigma$.

**The input tensors**, in the order of the graph's inputs (`encode.INPUTS`). Every list is padded to the longest in the batch, and to at least 1, with a mask of 1 for what is present and 0 for padding:

| name | shape | type | content |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cards` | $B \times n$ | int | each card's table entry |
| `ups` | $B \times n$ | float | its upgrade level, 0 to 3 |
| `card_mask` | $B \times n$ | float | present or padding |
| `mods` | $B \times n \times m$ | int | its modifier tokens' table entries, $m$ the most a card has |
| `mod_mask` | $B \times n \times m$ | float | which modifier tokens are present |
| `mod_amounts` | $B \times n \times m$ | float | its enchantment's and affliction's amounts / 10, in their modifiers' places, capped at ±10; 0 elsewhere |
| `numbers` | $B \times n \times 2$ | float | its damage / 20 and its block / 20, capped at ±10 |
| `relics`, `relic_mask` | $B \times r$ | int, float | each relic's entry, and its mask |
| `potions`, `potion_mask` | $B \times p$ | int, float | each potion's entry, and its mask |
| `context`, `context_mask` | $B \times c$ | int, float | each context token's entry, and its mask |
| `map`, `map_mask` | $B \times m$ | int, float | each map token's entry, and its mask |
| `state` | $B \times 6$ | float | the six numbers, standardized |
| `relic_values` | $B \times r \times 3$ | float | each relic's counter / 10, whether one shows (unused by the graph), whether it is melted |
| `relic_status` | $B \times r$ | int | each relic's status's table entry |
| `held`, `held_mask` | $B \times l$ | int, float | each card the relics keep, its entry in the `cards` table, and its mask |

## 3. The deck

A deck is a *multiset*: the order of its cards means nothing, and its size varies from about 10 to 40. Reading it in a sequence would make the net learn that every order is the same; a vector of counts per card would have hundreds of mostly empty slots and nothing shared between similar cards. So each card goes through the same small network on its own, and the results are added up: a Deep Set (Zaheer et al., 2017).

**The card's vector.** A card with id $c$, upgrade level $u$, damage $d$, block $k$ and modifiers $m$ with amounts $a_m$ becomes 64 numbers:

$$
x = E[c] + u\, U[c] + d\, D[c] + k\, K[c] + \sum_{m} \big(M[m] + a_m A[m]\big).
$$

$E$, $U$ and $M$ are learned tables (*embeddings*): one vector per card id, one *upgrade direction* per card id, and one vector per modifier. Upgrading moves a card along a direction learned for that card, so upgrades can matter a lot for one card and little for another. $D$, $K$ and $A$ are directions too, one per card id for its damage and its block and one per modifier for its amount: a number moves only its own card or modifier, so V learns what a grown Scythe or a large Goopy is worth without a rule that reads every card's damage alike. For a card whose numbers its id and upgrade fix, they say nothing new, and its rows can stay where they are. Each entry starts as random numbers of standard deviation 0.1.

**$\varphi$, the card's network.** Two layers, the same weights for every card:

$$
\varphi(x) = \mathrm{ReLU}\big(W_2\, \mathrm{ReLU}(W_1 x + b_1) + b_2\big),
$$

with $W_1$ of 64 → 256 and $W_2$ of 256 → 256. ReLU is $\max(0, x)$, number by number, so all 256 outputs are at least 0. Here $\varphi$ sees one card: two copies of a card give the same vector, and no card knows what else is in the deck.

**Pooling.** The vectors are added up, padded slots left out, and read two ways:

$$
\bar h = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n} \varphi(x_k), \qquad \tilde h = \frac{1}{16}\sum_{k=1}^{n} \varphi(x_k).
$$

The mean says what the deck is made of. It alone would read a deck and that deck doubled as the same, so the scaled sum says how much of it there is (the factor $1/16$ keeps it about a mean's size). The two are one vector at two scales, so the 512 numbers hold 256 numbers' worth of direction plus the deck's size.

**How cards relate.** Because the pooled vector is a sum, a relation between cards is not computed in $\varphi$. It comes in two steps: $\varphi$ can give a kind of card a number of its own, so that the sum counts those cards, and the body after the join can then combine such counts with each other and with the relics. "Many Shivs and a Shuriken" takes only that. The cost of this is in [§8](#sec-8).

**The cards the relics keep.** A relic can hold cards out of the deck: Pael's Tooth takes cards from the deck when it is picked up, and gives one back, upgraded, after each fight won. Each such card is its id's vector $E[c]$ alone, from the deck's table, through the same $\varphi$, and the results are summed with no scaling:

$$
\hat h = \sum_{c \in \text{kept}} \varphi\big(E[c]\big).
$$

These 256 numbers are a part of the join of their own, apart from the deck's, so the net can tell a card held by a relic from one in the deck. No kept card gives the zero vector.

## 4. The bags: relics, potions, context, map

Each of the four is a **bag**: every token has a learned vector of 64 numbers, and the vectors are added up. There is no layer in between and no mean. A token that appears twice counts twice.

$$
\text{bag}(t_1, \dots, t_k) = \sum_{i=1}^{k} T[t_i].
$$

Each bag has its own table $T$: relic ids, potion ids, context tokens and map tokens. An empty bag is the zero vector.

**A relic** adds what the combat net reads of it to its id's vector: its counter $q$ (/ 10) times a learned vector of its id, a learned vector $W$ if it is melted ($\mu = 1$), and its status $s$'s vector,

$$
T[r] + q\, T_q[r] + \mu\, W + T_s[s],
$$

so the relics' bag sums these. A counter that runs down (a relic's charges) or a status that changes (an effect used) moves the relic's vector along a direction of its own.

**The map's tokens** (`encode.map_tokens`). The player's point is the last one visited, or the start (the first point by row, then column) before any. From there, the net follows the map's links to every point a path still leads to, and each becomes one token: its type and how many floors ahead it is, as `Elite+3`, with `:quest` added if a quest marks it. So the bag says how many of each kind of room lie at each distance. It holds no links ([§8](#sec-8)).

**The point being entered.** On the map, V compares options by valuing the afterstate of each: the point is added to the path, and nothing else changes ([Search §1](/en/arch/macro-brain/search/#sec-1)). The points after two options are often the same, so the options must differ by the point itself. An afterstate of a map move is therefore marked (`MapView.Entering`), and its bag starts with the point entered, as `Elite+0`.

The mark means *about to enter*, and only that. The room the player already stands in is no token, for a reason that matters: a run view logged in a room comes after its combat, and a run that died there logs none. A token for the current room would be read, in training, only by the runs that survived it, and V would take entering an elite for having beaten it.

So each map move also gives a training sample of its own, the chosen point's afterstate with that run's real outcome ([From hazards to value §3](/en/arch/macro-brain/value/#sec-3)). V then learns what entering an elite at this HP is worth, deaths in that elite included.

## 5. The body and the heads

**The numbers** are read as they are, standardized ([§2](#sec-2)).

**The join.** The seven outputs are concatenated into one vector $z$ of $512 + 64 + 64 + 64 + 64 + 6 + 256 = 1030$ numbers: the deck, the four bags, the numbers and the cards the relics keep.

**The body** is an MLP of two layers, 512 numbers each:

$$
b_1 = \mathrm{ReLU}(W_3 z + c_1), \qquad b_2 = \mathrm{ReLU}(W_4 b_1 + c_2).
$$

This is where everything meets. The deck has seen no relic, the map has seen no HP; whether a path with two elites suits this deck at this HP is the body's to work out.

**The hazard head** is one linear layer on $b_2$, followed by a sigmoid: for each floor $g$ from 0 to 63, a *hazard*

$$
h_g = \sigma(w_g^\top b_2 + a_g),
$$

the chance that the run ends on floor $g$ if it is still going there. The value is the chance to survive every floor from the run's current floor $f$ on:

$$
\text{P(win)} = \prod_{g = f}^{63} (1 - h_g).
$$

The hazards of floors before $f$ are computed and not used ([From hazards to value §2](/en/arch/macro-brain/value/#sec-2)).

**The second head** is one linear layer on $b_2$ of 6 outputs, used only in training. It predicts the next combats' HP costs, which shapes the body; it never decides, and it is not in the net file ([From hazards to value §4](/en/arch/macro-brain/value/#sec-4)).

## 6. Sizes and cost

| part | shape | weights |
|---|---|---|
| card, upgrade, damage, block, modifier, amount embeddings | 4 × 597 × 64, 2 × 57 × 64 | 160,128 |
| relic, potion, context, map embeddings | 310, 66, 32, 379 × 64 | 50,368 |
| relic counter, status, melted | 310 × 64; 5 × 64; 64 | 20,224 |
| $\varphi$ | 64 → 256 → 256 | 82,432 |
| body | 1030 → 512 → 512 | 790,528 |
| hazard head | 512 → 64 | 32,832 |
| **total** | | **1,136,512** |

The token tables are fixed, so these sizes depend only on the game build. The training-only head adds 3,078.

**What the file holds.** The champion's file is `weights/all/v9-2.json`. It learned from SL self-play of all five characters at ascension 0, at the medium budget: 21,272 runs, 5,608 of them won, 4,310 of them held out (its `fit`, [From hazards to value §6](/en/arch/macro-brain/value/#sec-6)). The damage, block and amount rows ($D$, $K$, $A$) have learned wherever a training run gave a card such a number: 170 cards' damage rows, 68 cards' block rows and 20 enchantments' amount rows. The other rows of the three are zero, so V gives those numbers no weight. So are the relics' counter, status and melted vectors, and the body's 256 columns that read the cards the relics keep: V gives these inputs no weight either. Its $\mu$ and $\sigma$ come from runs at ascension 0: the ascension's $\mu$ is 0 and its $\sigma$ reads as 1, so A10 reads as 10. An id that no training run held, such as `SHIV`, the afflictions or the `FAKE_` relics, has learned nothing: its row is still the one it started with.

A call takes about 0.08 ms in C# on one thread, batch of one. A macro decision on the clone takes 70–90 ms, most of it in the game ([Search §6](/en/arch/macro-brain/search/#sec-6)), so V's own cost does not matter.

## 7. One forward pass, two implementations

The trainer computes the net in PyTorch; the brain runs it in C#. They must agree exactly, and they do so through the net file (`weights/all/NAME.json`, [Learning: reference §7](/en/arch/learning/reference/#sec-7)):

- **Export.** `learn/v/export.py` writes the net as an ONNX graph inside the file, with the sigmoid included, the inputs named as in [§2](#sec-2) and every length left free. Next to it are the token tables, $\mu$ and $\sigma$, the training report and 8 held-out samples with the values the trainer gave them.
- **Inference.** `RunNet` (`src/Sts2Ai.Brain/Macro/RunNet.cs`) follows `encode.py`: its `Tokens` and state, and the combat net's `CombatNet.RelicInputs` for the relics' numbers and statuses, build the same tensors (`Hazards`, `:174-221`), and ONNX Runtime runs the graph. `Value` multiplies the hazards from the current floor on.
- **The check.** `RunNet.Load` refuses the file unless it gives the 8 samples the trainer's values, to within 0.1% (and $10^{-6}$ for the smallest values, where the floats' noise is larger). A difference in how either side reads a run stops the file from loading. A value of P(win) can be a few hundredths, late in a hard run, and a tolerance that was not relative would pass nearly anything there.

## 8. What the structure cannot express

These follow from the structure alone.

- **No card sees another before the sum.** A relation between cards reaches the body only as numbers that survive adding up. Counting a kind of card is easy; a relation among specific cards has to find numbers of its own, among 256 shared by all.
- **The relics' bag is linear.** 310 relics share 64 numbers, with no layer before the join. A relation between two relics, or a relic and a card, is the body's alone to work out.
- **A kept card is its id.** The run view gives the cards a relic keeps by id alone, so their modifiers and numbers, which a card of the deck carries, are not read.
- **The map bag holds no links.** It lists what lies at each distance, not which points lead to which. An elite and a rest site on two different paths read just like both on one path. The choice in front of the player is seen through each option's own bag; a choice further ahead is not.
- **The map ends with the act.** The net reads the current act's remaining points. Of later acts it knows only what the floor implies; their maps, bosses and ancients are not in the run view's map.
- **What is behind is gone.** Points already passed and points off every path ahead are not tokens. What the run gained there is in its loadout, HP and gold.
- **Ids, not rules.** The net knows a card only by the runs whose decks held it. A card the training data never held has learned nothing, whatever it does.
- **One number for each kind of risk.** The hazards say how likely the run is to end on each floor, not why. A single head has to carry every way of dying.
