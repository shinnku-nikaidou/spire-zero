In NoSL the combat brain knows only what a player knows: the screen, and what can be inferred from it. It does not know the order of the draw pile, what a random effect will roll, or which of its moves a monster has queued behind its intent. It cannot plan on the real fight, because it may not look at it.

So it plans on fights it builds itself. **PIMC** (perfect-information Monte Carlo) draws $K$ complete worlds, hidden parts included, each of which agrees with everything the player has seen; searches each one as an ordinary game with nothing hidden; and averages the results. The answer is the action that is best on average over the worlds. The NoSL champion plays it with 8 worlds of 300 nodes each and the combat net c2 as its leaf (`run --policy pimc`).

This chapter is in three sections: what is hidden and how the worlds are made (this page), the search in each world and the average ([Search](/en/arch/pimc/search/)), and the budget and results ([Budget](/en/arch/pimc/budget/)). Where the idea sits among the environments is in [Environments](/en/arch/environments/).

## 1. What NoSL cannot see

The **hidden state** $s$ of a fight is three things: the draw pile's order, the position of every random stream (the game draws all its randomness from seeded streams), and, for each monster, which move it has queued among those that show its current intent. The **public record** $h$ is the observation plus the intents each enemy has shown at the start of each round ([Combat brain: prompts, moves and workers §2](/en/arch/combat-brain/shared/#sec-2)).

The monsters are the subtle part. Each picks its moves with a move state machine in the game's code, mixing three kinds of rule:

- a **fixed cycle** (attack, defend, attack, …; or charge, then the big hit), deterministic given the moves so far;
- a **weighted roll with constraints**: 60% attack and 40% buff, say, but never the same move three times in a row, or one move always followed by another;
- a **condition**: another move set below half HP, or enemies taking turns.

The rolls draw from the monster's own random stream, which the run's seed fixes: the same seed gives the same moves, which SL may use. A player never sees the stream, so a roll stays unknown until its intent shows. This round's move is always known, since its intent shows. Players learn a monster quickly because the constraints leave the rolls little room, and what stays unknown is exactly the rolls. The combat net shows the same picture: from the record it names the next round's intent type for 57.7% of the enemies, against 28.9% for repeating the current one, and its misses fall mostly on the rolls ([Combat brain: the leaf §3](/en/arch/combat-brain/leaf/#sec-3)).

## 2. Sampled worlds

A world is one sample of $s$ that agrees with $h$: a complete fight, built in the worker's own copy of the game, that shows exactly the observation the player sees. Two steps make it.

**Build** (`CombatBuilder.Setup(observation, seed)`, [Simulator: combats §5](/en/arch/simulator/combat/#sec-5)) makes a fight that shows the observation:

1. start the encounter with the observation's deck, relics and HP;
2. overwrite the visible state (`Apply`): the round, the enemies (added, removed, reordered), HP and block, energy, gold, potions, orbs, relic counters, powers with their stacks, the four piles with each card's modifications, and the intents;
3. draw the hidden state (`Resample`);
4. compare the rebuilt fight's observation with the input, path by path (`BuildDifferences`): empty means exact.

What the screen shows is often a function of numbers it does not show, different for each power and relic, so a **knob solver** tries the internal numbers (amounts, fields, a power's private data) until the display matches. The intents are matched by choosing, for each monster, among the moves that show them, and each monster's **move log** is rebuilt from the public record, so that its state machine goes on as the real one would: an earlier round matches the move that showed the same intents (else the same types and hits, else the same types).

**Determinize** (`Determinize(seed)`, [Simulator: combats §3](/en/arch/simulator/combat/#sec-3)) then replaces everything the player cannot know with a sample, and leaves the visible state as it is:

- every random stream is reseeded;
- the draw pile is first sorted by card key, an order that depends only on what the player sees, then shuffled (Fisher–Yates);
- the cards outside the hand are renumbered, since the game numbers cards in draw order and the old ids would leak it;
- each monster's queued move is drawn uniformly among the moves that show the same intents.

World $k$ is the built fight plus `Determinize(seed_k)`, with $\text{seed}_k$ mixed from the search's seed and $k$. All of it, the cards, the rolls and the enemies' turn, is then played by the real game code; no net simulates anything, and the leaf only values where a line stops.

The figure takes a real NoSL decision, the first turn of an elite fight against Terror Eel ([Search §3](/en/arch/pimc/search/#sec-3)), and puts its draw pile in order the same way: sorted, then shuffled with each world's seed. These are drawn in the page with its own generator, so they are not the 8 worlds the run searched, but they are worlds of the same kind. The chance that a card is among the next draws, over many worlds, approaches the exact one; over 8 it is rough, which is the noise of [Search §3](/en/arch/pimc/search/#sec-3).

## 3. How faithful a world is

A world built from the observation should play like the real fight, apart from what `Determinize` samples on purpose. `fidelity` measures it: at every second decision of a panel's fights, the real fight plays on with a placeholder policy to the end of the turn, the enemies' turn included, and a fight built from the decision's observation and record is given the real one's sampled state and plays the same actions; the first observation that differs is counted ([Simulator: combats §6](/en/arch/simulator/combat/#sec-6)). With Silent's starter deck (2026-10-27):

| fights | decisions | the same through the enemies' turn, without the record → with it |
|---|---|---|
| act 0 hallway, 30 | 459 | 82.4% → 96.3%; 97.4% with stuns' follow-ups |
| act 1 hallway, 30 | 478 | 88.7% → 98.1%; 99.0% with stuns' follow-ups |
| act 0 boss, 9 | 201 | 96.5% → 96.5% |
| act 0 elite, 15 | 289 | 99.0% → 99.0% |

No fight was built wrong, and no action was illegal in a built one. Within the turn at most 1% differ. The rest differ after the turn ends, on counters inside a few monsters (a rat's summons, a giant's growing damage) that no observation shows. The record of moves is what lifts the hallway fights from 82–89% to 96–98%. What the builder still approximates: this turn's history and the counters inside powers and monsters, how long cost modifiers last, the player's debuffs wearing off, and a deck taken as every card seen.

In whole runs a world built wrong fails the decision. Five of the first 400 PIMC runs failed that way: a spent Silver Crucible's hidden counter (three runs), a Glass orb's numbers, and Sloth's limit, which only its tooltip shows (the world allowed fewer cards a turn than the real fight, and a legal play was illegal in it). All three are fixed ([Simulator: combats §6](/en/arch/simulator/combat/#sec-6)).

## 4. Fair play

NoSL's rule is that a decision depends only on the public record ([Environments](/en/arch/environments/)). The search is built so that it cannot break it:

- **Only the observation leaves the host.** A worker gets the observation, the record and the seeds, never the true fight ([Combat brain: prompts, moves and workers §3](/en/arch/combat-brain/shared/#sec-3)). Every world is built from the observation, never from the real fight, so the hidden state in a world is the world's own sample, and nothing in it is the real game's.
- **The seeds are the caller's.** `Determinize` takes its seed from the search, never from the run.
- **The leaf reads the observation.** Neither `LeafValue` nor the combat net reads hidden state ([Combat brain: the leaf §1](/en/arch/combat-brain/leaf/#sec-1)).
- **The same path everywhere.** The first rung of the evaluation ladder, the simulator, builds its worlds the same way the mod will from the real game: no shortcut determinizes the true state for decisions ([Evaluation §2](/en/arch/evaluation/#sec-2)). Determinizing the true state stays for tests and benchmarks only.
- **Planned:** a counterfactual test, the same public record with a different hidden state, which must give the same choice for a fixed brain seed.
