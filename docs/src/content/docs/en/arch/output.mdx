The brain answers a decision with an action, a plan and the options it scored. The **output layer** turns that answer into something the player sees, or, when the player asks, something the game does. It runs inside the [mod](/en/arch/mod/), in the game process, and every output mode reads the same `Recommendation`: the brain never knows which mode is on. This page is for whoever wants to change what the player sees. [Mod: the main loop](/en/arch/mod/loop/) says when an answer is asked for and how long it is shown.

<Aside title="Status">

Built: the **panel** ([§2](#sec-2)), a card over the game with a toolbar that lets the AI play, V's chance to win the run, the next action with how the fight or the run is expected to go after it, the plan after it, and the options the brain scored with their chances to win (on the map with each option's route); its **settings**, in an "AI" tab the mod adds to the game's settings screen; the mod's words in six languages; two kinds of **marks** ([§3](#sec-3)): on the map, a ring on the recommended point and a line along the route the brain plans after it, up to the boss; in combat, the plan's step numbers on the cards it plays this turn and on its targets and the end turn button; and **auto** ([§4](#sec-4)), the AI playing the answers when the player asks. Planned: highlights on the other surfaces, narration ([§5](#sec-5)), and the facts it needs. Godot node classes (`N*` types) are cited by class name only: each name is unique in the decompiled source, so find the file by name. Background notes are in the local note `tmp/notes/output-design.md`.

</Aside>

## 1. The contract

The output reads one message, `Recommendation` ([Protocol: the wire §7](/en/arch/protocol/wire/#sec-7)), as built (`Sts2Ai.Protocol/Wire.cs:64-65`):

| field | what | from whom |
|---|---|---|
| `decision_id`, `obs_hash` | the decision it answers | |
| `action` | the action to take now | every brain |
| `brain` | the brain that answered, as Harness names it ([Harness: routing, budgets and seeds §1](/en/arch/harness/routing/#sec-1)) | |
| `plan`? | what the brain planned after `action` | PIMC: the rest of the turn, in the first world; the SL planner: the rest of the combat; L0 on the map: the route to the boss; no other screen, and no card prompt |
| `candidates`? | the options the brain scored, the chosen first: each with its value, the half-width of its 95% confidence interval and its sample count; from the SL planner also the chance to win the fight and the HP left; on the map also the option's route ([Protocol: the wire §7](/en/arch/protocol/wire/#sec-7)) | the combat brains; L0 outside combat (V's chance to win the run), not when it explored |

`plan` comes *after* `action`: the next step is `action`, and `plan[0]` is the one after it.

**The consumers.** The brain never knows which mode is on.

| mode | consumer | state |
|---|---|---|
| panel (assist, simple) | one card for every surface ([§2](#sec-2)) | built |
| marks (assist) | the map's ring and route; the combat plan's numbers and rings ([§3](#sec-3)) | built |
| toolbar | the AI plays `action`: one step, to the end of the turn or the room, or on its own ([§4](#sec-4)) | built |
| auto | the executor plays `action`, once per state, then the mod observes again ([§4](#sec-4), [Mod: auto-play and safety §1](/en/arch/mod/auto/#sec-1)) | built |
| highlight (assist, advanced) | the other surfaces' nodes, found as the executor finds their buttons ([§3](#sec-3)) | planned |

A *surface* is the kind of decision screen being shown (combat, map, shop, a card prompt and so on). An *anchor* is the Godot node on screen that an action belongs to, such as the card holder of the card to play.

**Stale answers.** An answer is matched to the open decision by its id, and kept for the key it was asked for: the observation's hash and its legal actions. It is drawn only on the frames that key lasts; when the screen moves on, the panel shows "Thinking…" for the new state, and the mod asks again ([Mod: the main loop §3](/en/arch/mod/loop/#sec-3)). The mod never reads the answer's `obs_hash`.

**Planned.**

- **Facts**: the numbers an explanation may use, as a field of the recommendation; the only source narration would have ([§5](#sec-5)). No `Facts` exist in the code.
- **Anytime answers**: in assist mode, the brain sending its best answer so far and then better ones, each replacing the last ([Harness: routing, budgets and seeds §4](/en/arch/harness/routing/#sec-4)). Today the brain sends one answer per decision, and the mod clears the decision at the first.

## 2. The panel

The panel is a card over the game, near its top left corner (`UI/Panel.cs`). It has its own `CanvasLayer`, a Godot node that draws its children on a separate layer: layer 100, above the game's UI, which every mark shares. The card takes no clicks but its buttons', so the game under it gets them. F8 hides and shows it with every mark.

<PanelMock client:visible lang="en" />

**The layout**, top to bottom (`UI/Panel.cs`):

- **The card**: at (24, 132), at least 340 px wide, 12 px of padding (14 at the sides), corner radius 14, a dark purple border, a shadow, and a near-black fill at the opacity setting. A long line can widen it. Its text is 11 to 17 px, in the game's own font for its language (`UI/Fonts.cs`), as are the combat marks' numbers: the game's substitute font for Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Russian and Thai, else Kreon. The game lays out a 1920×1080 canvas and stretches it to the window, with font oversampling off; its own fonts are MSDF fonts and stay sharp at any scale, where the engine's default font would be drawn at 1080p and stretched, blurred.
- **The head**: a purple "AI" badge; the champion's name and, under it, the status, each one line cut with an ellipsis where its space ends; the SL/NoSL switch, the selected side filled purple (neither side in multiplayer, and no switch when the mod is off); and "F8". The status is the run's own line once the brain has taken the run: character, ascension, floor and HP ([Mod §6](/en/arch/mod/#sec-6)).
- **The toolbar**, in a debugger's style, once the brain has taken the run: three buttons, 32 px tall, each an icon and a word. The mod draws the icons itself (`UI/Icons.cs`: play, pause, step, finish) at three times the size they are shown at, 15 px, so the canvas's scaling does not blur them. The first is "Auto" (F5), or "Pause" while auto-play runs; the second plays one step (F10): "Play one" in combat, "Step" on the map, "Pick for me" elsewhere; the third finishes (F11): "Finish turn", "Next room", "Finish room". While auto-play runs the other two are off ([Mod: auto-play and safety §2](/en/arch/mod/auto/#sec-2)).
- **The effort row**, under the toolbar: "Effort" and five toggles, the brain's own and the SL planner's four presets ([Mod §5](/en/arch/mod/#sec-5)).
- **While auto-play runs**, two more rows: "Speed", slow, medium or fast; and "Stop at", two toggles, "elites, bosses" and "win chance -3".
- **The strip**: "WIN CHANCE" and V's chance to win the run, in large type, with its change since the answer outside combat before, in points: "+0.6" in green or "-2.6" in red. On its right a pill with the AI's state: "Your move", "Auto on" (a green dot), "Acting" while a goal plays, or "AI stopped: REASON" in amber. The chance is the value of the chosen option at the latest answer outside combat (V's, [Macro brain: search §1](/en/arch/macro-brain/search/#sec-1)), so it moves at each decision outside combat and holds through a fight; before the run's first such answer the strip has only the pill.
- **An answer**: "NEXT", then the next action after a filled "1" badge, in large type (on the map a ring instead of the badge). Under it, a quieter line: outside combat "win chance 65.7%", V's chance to win after this action; in combat "this fight 100% · 64 HP left", the SL planner's chance to win the fight on its chosen line and the HP held at the win, kept while the plan is followed. Then, on the map, "ROUTE TO THE BOSS" and the route's rooms joined by dots, unless the options below show the routes; elsewhere the plan's steps, numbered from 2 in ring badges; then the plan's later turns, "6 more turns planned" ([Mod: the main loop §6](/en/arch/mod/loop/#sec-6)).
- **The considered options**, under a rule: up to five of the options the brain scored, the chosen first, in lilac, then best first. The columns depend on what the values are:

  | answer | columns |
  |---|---|
  | a combat the SL planner answered (its candidates carry `win`) | "win", the chance to win the fight on the option's best line, in whole percent; "HP left", the HP held at that win, or "—" with no chance to win |
  | outside combat (L0, valued by V) | "win", V's chance to win the run after the option, to one decimal; "diff", its gap to the best option in points, "—" for the chosen and the best |
  | a combat PIMC answered | "value ±CI", the mean score to three decimals and the half-width of its 95% interval |

  With bars, each row also has a 56 px track with a short mark at the value, the chosen one's purple; for PIMC the mark spans the interval, with a tick at the value. The rows share one scale: from the lowest value to the highest (intervals included), widened by 15% of that span, and at least half a point, on each side; a fight's scale reaches 100%. A PIMC answer shows the list only when it scored more than one action; the other two show even one option. A step played from a followed plan has no options.
- **On the map** each option is one row under "ROUTE TO THE BOSS": the route as 16 px room chips, from the option's own point to the boss, then its chance to win and its gap, with no bars; under the rows, one legend of the room types shown, with the map legend's names. A chip is a small square outlined in its type's color with the first letter of its legend name ("?" for an unknown room), the boss's filled red; the chosen route's plain rooms are brighter than the others'. A potion of the belt, which the map offers too, has no route: its row is a single "?" chip.
- **The foot**: the brain that answered and the seconds the mod waited for it, from sending to receiving.
- **A message** instead of an answer: a dot, the message, and a quieter second line; amber for something the player can fix or wait out, red for an error. Every message is listed in [Mod §6](/en/arch/mod/#sec-6).
- **Recent**: once the AI has played, "RECENT" and its last four actions, newest first: the floor, the action, and its answer's value, V's chance to win or the fight's.

The strip and the recent actions show with a message too. The body is built again only when what it shows changes, or a setting it is drawn with; the toolbar's labels follow a change of the game's language at once; the marks follow their nodes every frame.

**Settings.** The player's choices are kept in `~/.local/state/sts2ai/settings.json`, beside the brain's socket and outside the game's profile; every row, its values and its default are in [Mod §5](/en/arch/mod/#sec-5). The panel and the marks read the settings live, so a change applies at once.

**The AI tab** (`UI/SettingsTab.cs`) sits in the game's settings screen after General, Graphics, Sound and Input. The game builds its four tabs in `NSettingsTabManager._Ready` from nodes in its scene. A postfix there, run at the next idle so the screen is whole, copies the Input tab for the tab's button and the Sound panel, emptied, for its panel, so both look and switch like the game's own; the copies take the nodes and their scripts, not the game's signal connections. It moves the tab row half a step left so it stays centred, wires the button to the manager's own tab switch, and adds the pair to the manager's tabs. The rows are the mod's own controls in the font of the game's settings rows, under two headers, "Assist" and "Auto-play": `<  value  >` steppers and an opacity slider, and a button that resets them. Everything is built before any of it joins the screen, so if building fails, the screen stays as the game built it and the error is logged.

**Languages** (`UI/L10n.cs`). The mod's own words follow the language the game is set to: English, French, Spanish (both of the game's Spanish languages), Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese and Japanese; any other language reads English. A line is looked up by its English text, which is also what a line missing from the table shows. Names (cards, relics, potions, monsters, characters, events, rest options, the map legend's room types) come from the game's own tables instead ([Mod: the main loop §6](/en/arch/mod/loop/#sec-6)). What the mod does not write stays as it comes: the brain's name and its error messages, an exception's text, and the version guard's line.

**Planned for the panel:** 1–2 facts per answer (expected HP loss against the alternative, lethal next turn), the only numbers narration would use ([§5](#sec-5)); and the per-surface highlights of [§3](#sec-3), such as each card reward's chance to win on its card. A zero-UI variant, where the brain serves a local web page for a second screen, was considered for the earliest testing only.

## 3. Highlight

**The follow overlay.** Marks are drawn by the mod's own nodes, which live only in the mod's `CanvasLayer`, on top of the game. Each frame, each mark reads its target node's transform on screen (`GetGlobalTransformWithCanvas()`) with the node's size, so it follows a card that turns, fans out or scales, and moves with it; when the target is gone or hidden, the mark hides. The mod never writes into the game's nodes to draw, for two reasons: `NHandCardHolder.UpdateCard()` resets `CardHighlight` on every state change (`Nodes.Cards.Holders/NHandCardHolder.cs:613-640`) <Badge text="verified" variant="success" />, and the cyan, gold and red glows already mean something in the game (`NCardHighlight`). The one place the mod changes the game's nodes to draw is the settings screen ([§2](#sec-2)); when the AI plays, the executor presses the game's own buttons as a click does ([Mod: auto-play and safety §1](/en/arch/mod/auto/#sec-1)).

**The style**: purple, a color the game does not use for its own glows: outlines, rings and round purple badges with plain digits and a dark ring.

**On the map** (`UI/MapMark.cs`): a pulsing purple ring on the next point, and a purple line along each path of the route after it, from where the player is to the route's end. The map screen keeps its point nodes and each path's dots in private dictionaries, which a new act replaces; a line runs from a path's first dot to its last, so it stops short of the points' icons. The ring's radius is half the point's larger side plus 14, scaled with the map, and pulses by ±10% once a second. The route setting shows the whole route, only the ring, or nothing:

<MapRoute client:visible lang="en" />

**In combat** (`UI/CombatMark.cs`): the plan as the panel shows it, from `action` to this turn's end.

- each card the plan plays is outlined in purple on its holder's hitbox, with its step's number at its top right corner;
- each target, an enemy or an ally, is outlined on its creature node's hitbox, 10 px outside it, with the numbers of every step aimed at it in one badge at the top edge's middle;
- the end turn button is outlined with its step's number.

A card or creature that is gone has no mark. "Order numbers on cards" turns the card outlines and their numbers off together; "Target and end-turn marks" the rest. A potion has no mark of its own, but its target gets its step's number.

**Anchors.** Each surface's node, verified in the game's source (local note `tmp/notes/output-design.md` §3.2) <Badge text="verified" variant="success" />:

| surface | node | mark | state |
|---|---|---|---|
| hand | `NPlayerHand.Instance.GetCardHolder(card).Hitbox`, the card from `NetCombatCardDb` by its uid | outline + step number | built |
| target | `NCombatRoom.Instance.GetCreatureNode(creature).Hitbox` | outline + the steps' numbers | built |
| end turn | `NCombatRoom.Instance.Ui.EndTurnButton` | outline + step number | built |
| map | `NMapPoint` in `NMapScreen`, and our own `Line2D` along its paths' dots (not `NMapDrawings`, which is shared in multiplayer) | ring + route | built |
| potion | `NPotionHolder` | badge | planned |
| in-combat card select | hand select mode, `NCombatPileCardSelectScreen` | check marks | planned |
| card reward | `NCardRewardSelectionScreen` holders + skip | ΔP per card, best outlined | planned |
| rewards | `NRewardButton` | claim order / skip | planned |
| event | `NEventOptionButton` | best outlined, ΔP on the others | planned |
| rest | `NRestSiteButton` | same | planned |
| shop | `NMerchantCard/Relic/Potion/CardRemoval` | buy order | planned |
| grid select | `NCardGridSelectionScreen` family, `NCardGrid` | check marks | planned |

**Order of work:** hand and targets (done); card reward; map (done); event, rest and shop; grid prompts.

**Pitfalls:**

- Holder nodes are pooled (reused) and re-parented, and they animate: the follow overlay handles this by looking them up every frame.
- Screen resolution, UI scale and controller input: only screen transforms, never local positions.
- The brain plans again after every action, so the numbers start again from 1 at each answer.

## 4. Auto

When the player asks, the AI plays the answers itself (`Assist.cs`, [Mod: auto-play and safety §2](/en/arch/mod/auto/#sec-2)). The loop runs on the mod's per-frame loop ([Mod: the main loop §1](/en/arch/mod/loop/#sec-1)):

<Steps>

1. Wait until the decision on screen has held for 3 frames.
2. Ask the brain, or take its answer for this key: the observation's hash and its legal actions.
3. If the goal is reached (the turn is over, the room is left and a map decision is next), stop; with auto-play, stop before an elite or a boss and when V's chance to win has fallen, if the player keeps those stops.
4. Wait the speed's delay since the answer showed: 1.5, 0.8 or 0.2 s.
5. Play `action` through the executor, and only that, once for this key ([Mod: auto-play and safety §1](/en/arch/mod/auto/#sec-1)); send `executed`.
6. The next state is a new key: repeat. A key that still holds 10 s after its action stops the AI.

</Steps>

Each action is a new decision: the brain is asked again after every one, because draws and random results feed the next. A brain that follows a plan answers from it while the game shows what the plan expected, with no search ([The SL planner §4](/en/arch/sl-planner/#sec-4)). The game's own speed setting is left as it is; the planned auto-run driver would set it to Instant ([Mod: auto-play and safety §2](/en/arch/mod/auto/#sec-2)).

## 5. Narration <Badge text="planned" variant="caution" />

Narration is the text that would explain a recommendation.

- It is built only from the facts and the candidates, never from free reasoning about the state. NoSL facts are probabilities and expectations; SL facts may be exact ("A then B draws X").
- Templates come first, one per kind of fact.
- Optionally, a cheap LLM polishes the text, offline or asynchronously (the project's LLM gateway, cheap models only). Its input is the facts as JSON and the template's text. Its output is checked: every number in the text must equal a fact's value (a string match after formatting); if not, the template's text is shown instead.
- The LLM never sees game state beyond the facts, and never affects the action.

Nothing of it is built: the recommendation carries no facts yet ([§1](#sec-1)).

## 6. Fair play in output

| rule | state |
|---|---|
| The AI plays only when the player asks, and only the brain's answer to the decision on screen, once per state. The answer is checked against the decision's legal list by Harness ([Harness: routing, budgets and seeds §6](/en/arch/harness/routing/#sec-6)) | holds |
| Besides answers, auto-play presses only screens that are no decision: a chest, a proceed button, an ancient's dialogue ([Mod: auto-play and safety §1](/en/arch/mod/auto/#sec-1)) | holds |
| There is no interface for state edits, the console or reloads | holds |
| The socket carries only protocol messages. The SL export is sent only in SL, and carries the node's save, the seed and random streams included ([Mod: auto-play and safety §4](/en/arch/mod/auto/#sec-4)) | holds |
| The mod writes only its settings file and its lines in the game's log | holds |
| `NumReloads` is logged, and must be 0 | planned |

A new field of a protocol message gets a row here when it changes what may leave the game.
