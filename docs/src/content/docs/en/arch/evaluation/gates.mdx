A **gate** is the test a challenger must pass to replace the champion. This page explains the statistics a gate rests on, starting from nothing, then each gate, option by option. The numbers a gate reads are on [Metrics, seeds and reports](/en/arch/evaluation/metrics/); the file a champion is named in is on [Champions and baselines](/en/arch/evaluation/champions/).

## 1. Why a gate, and why pair

A challenger that looks better on a few runs may only have been lucky. Whole runs are slow (about five minutes each at medium effort) and noisy, so the question is how many runs it takes to tell a real gain from luck.

**Unpaired**, the answer is many. Two independent samples of runs, one per side, compared by win rate: at a win rate of about 30%, telling +5 pp from no difference takes about 1,300 runs per side, and +2 pp about 8,200 (two-sided α = 0.05, power 0.8, the variance 2p(1−p) of a difference of two rates). At a win rate of 7.7%, +3 pp takes about 1,240 runs per side.

Two ideas cut that down. Every gate here pairs; the whole-run SPRT also tests sequentially, while the wins gate plays a fixed set:

1. **Pair the runs by seed** ([§2](#sec-2)): play the challenger on the champion's seeds and compare seed by seed, so luck that both sides share drops out of the difference.
2. **Test sequentially** ([§3](#sec-3)): look after each pair and stop as soon as the evidence is clear, instead of fixing the number of runs in advance.

And gates are layered, from cheap and narrow to expensive and complete ([§5](#sec-5)).

## 2. Common random numbers

**The idea.** Common random numbers (CRN) means that both sides of a comparison meet the same random events, so that only the policy differs. Here it comes for free: a seed fixes the game, the run's own randomness comes from a hash of the seed ([Metrics, seeds and reports §6](/en/arch/evaluation/metrics/#sec-6)), and a run is a function of its setup and its answers ([Simulator: whole runs §6](/en/arch/simulator/runs/#sec-6)). So a challenger played on the champion's seeds faces the same map, the same first encounters, the same first card offers. Once the two make a different choice their games drift apart, but the map and much of what follows stay shared.

A seed fixes the whole run, so the champion's side never needs to be played again: its report already holds its runs, record by record, and a gate plays only the challenger.

**The paired difference.** For seed $i$, let $d_i$ be the challenger's floor minus the champion's. Over $n$ seeds the gain is estimated by the mean $\bar d$, with standard error $\mathrm{sd}(d)/\sqrt n$. Unpaired, the standard error of the difference of two means is $\sqrt{(s_a^2 + s_b^2)/n}$. Since $\mathrm{var}(d) = s_a^2 + s_b^2 - 2\,\mathrm{cov}(a, b)$, pairing gains exactly as much as the two sides' floors go together on the same seed.

**Measured** on committed reports (computed here; the code records no such number). A, B and C are three earlier nets, played in SL on `promotion-1-300`; D and E are two earlier NoSL champions, on `promotion-1`:

| pair, same seeds | n | correlation of floors | paired SE | unpaired SE | variance ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C − A, `promotion-1-300` | 300 | 0.34 | 0.618 | 0.757 | 1.50 |
| B − A, `promotion-1-300` | 300 | 0.23 | 0.695 | 0.790 | 1.29 |
| C − B, `promotion-1-300` | 300 | 0.36 | 0.687 | 0.855 | 1.55 |
| E − D, `promotion-1` | 200 | 0.46 | 0.627 | 0.853 | 1.85 |
| one V in SL and in NoSL, `promotion-1` | 200 | 0.60 | 0.548 | 0.865 | 2.49 |

The last row's two sides have different combat brains. A variance ratio of 1.5 means an unpaired comparison needs 1.5 times the runs per side for the same width. **On wins, pairing buys little while wins are rare**: C's 23 wins and A's 1 share no seed, and the paired standard error of the wins difference, 1.58 pp, is the unpaired one, 1.57 pp; C and B have 2 seeds in common among their 23 and 13. It helps once wins are common: two earlier nets on `promotion-1-500`, with 145 and 74 wins of which 35 on shared seeds, have a paired standard error of 2.36 pp against an unpaired 2.58 pp, a variance ratio of 1.19.

## 3. The sequential probability ratio test

**The idea.** A test with a fixed number of runs plays them all, even when the first fifty already settle the question. The **sequential probability ratio test** (SPRT, Wald 1945) looks after every pair and stops as soon as the evidence clearly favours one of two hypotheses about the true mean difference $\mu$:

- H0, the challenger is no better: $\mu = 0$;
- H1, it is better by at least $\delta$: $\mu = \delta$ ($\delta$ = 1.5 floors by default).

**The evidence** is the log-likelihood ratio: how much more likely the pairs so far are under H1 than under H0. If the differences are normal with variance $\sigma^2$,

$$
\Lambda_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \ln\frac{\varphi\big((d_i-\delta)/\sigma\big)}{\varphi\big(d_i/\sigma\big)} = \frac{\delta}{\sigma^2}\Big(S_n - \frac{n\delta}{2}\Big), \qquad S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n d_i .
$$

Each pair adds $\delta/\sigma^2 \cdot (d_i - \delta/2)$: a pair above $\delta/2$ pushes towards H1, one below pushes towards H0.

**The bounds.** Fix in advance $\alpha$, the chance to pass a challenger that is no better, and $\beta$, the chance to fail one that is better by $\delta$. Wald's bounds are

$$
A = \ln\frac{1-\beta}{\alpha}, \qquad B = \ln\frac{\beta}{1-\alpha};
$$

pass at the first $\Lambda_n \ge A$, fail at the first $\Lambda_n \le B$. At $\alpha = \beta = 0.05$, $A = \ln 19 = 2.944$ and $B = -2.944$. If the pairs run out first, the verdict is open. The bounds are Wald's approximations, and with the variance estimated as below the real error rates are not exactly α and β; they have not been measured ([§4](#sec-4)).

**As built** (`Gate.Sprt`, `Harness/Gate.cs:62-107`), for the `floor` metric:

- $\sigma^2$ is not known, so after each pair it is estimated from all pairs so far, ties included: $\hat\sigma^2 = \max\big(1, (Q_n - S_n^2/n)/(n-1)\big)$, with $Q_n = \sum d_i^2$.
- No LLR is computed before 10 pairs differ (`MinPairs`, `:25`). Tied pairs do not count towards the 10: a change that alters only some runs ties most pairs, and with ties alone the variance sits at its floor of 1, so counting them would fail such a change at the 10th pair. A change that lifts one run in ten by 15 floors (+1.5 on average) would fail that way whenever its first ten pairs tied, about 35% of the time (0.9¹⁰ = 0.35). `GateTests` has the case: with the rule it passes, after more than 100 pairs (after 110, by the figure's port below).
- It stops at the first pair past a bound; the result keeps that pair (`decided`), the LLR there and the mean difference over the pairs up to it (`floor_diff_mean`).

For the **`win` metric** only the pairs where exactly one side won count, as in McNemar's test ([§4](#sec-4)): $k$ won by the challenger alone, $m$ by the champion alone. If the challenger's share of those pairs is $p$, H0 is $p_0 = 0.5$ and H1 is $p_1 = 0.5 + \delta$ ($\delta$ = 0.1 by default), and

$$
\Lambda = k \ln\frac{p_1}{p_0} + m \ln\frac{1-p_1}{1-p_0} = 0.1823\,k - 0.2231\,m \quad (\delta = 0.1).
$$

A pass needs $k - 1.224\,m \ge 16.15$: 17 one-sided wins with none against. A fail needs $m - 0.817\,k \ge 13.2$. Ties, both won or both lost, add nothing, and there is no minimum.

**A real gate, replayed.** Four floor gates that each promoted a NoSL champion can be replayed from git: the champion's side is the report its champion file named before the promotion, and the challenger's is the promoted report, which `--promote` wrote from the gate's own records. The figure replays them with a port of `Gate.Sprt` and reaches each stored verdict at the stored pair, with the stored LLR to within 10⁻⁹: pass after 14, 77, 122 and 136 of 200 pairs. The last tab draws made-up pairs with a true difference you choose and runs the same gate on them, once or a thousand times.

**What a 200-seed gate can tell.** On made-up pairs like the last real gate's (47% of pairs tied, the others with an SD of 12 floors), the figure's 1,000 simulated gates give: with no true gain, 3.8% pass, 64% fail and 33% stay open; with a true gain of δ = 1.5 floors, 63% pass, 5% fail and 32% stay open; with 3 floors, 99.6% pass, after 72 pairs on average. At this noise the size of the seed set, more than α and β, limits the gate: a gain of δ is left open about a third of the time. The not-worse test, at a true loss of δ, passes 6.8% of the simulated gates, above its nominal 5%; the bounds are Wald's approximations and the variance is estimated.

## 4. McNemar's test and A/A tests

**McNemar's test** is the standard test for paired yes/no outcomes, such as a win on the same seed. Put each seed in one of four cells:

| | champion won | champion lost |
|---|---|---|
| **challenger won** | both | $k$ |
| **challenger lost** | $m$ | neither |

Seeds in "both" and "neither" say nothing about which side is better. Under no difference, each of the $k + m$ discordant seeds is a fair coin, so $k$ follows a binomial distribution with $k + m$ trials and chance one half, and an exact p-value follows from it. C against A of [§2](#sec-2), two earlier nets on `promotion-1-300`: $k$ = 23, $m$ = 1, none won by both; the chance of 23 or more of 24 under a fair coin is 25 / 2²⁴ ≈ 1.5 × 10⁻⁶.

No McNemar test exists in the code <Badge text="planned" variant="caution" />. What exists uses the same two counts: the C# win SPRT tests $k$ against $m$ sequentially ([§3](#sec-3)), and the wins gate bounds the mean of the per-seed differences, which depends on $k$, $m$ and the number of pairs ([§10](#sec-10)).

**An A/A test** runs a gate with the champion against itself. There is no real difference, so it must pass no more often than the gate's designed α; if it does, the gate is broken. Here, an A/A test on the same seeds is no test at all: a seed fixes the whole run, so the copy ties on every seed (one re-baseline played a champion again and all 200 seeds reached the same floor, [§9](#sec-9)), and a floor SPRT that never sees 10 differing pairs stays open. A useful A/A test would play the champion with its own randomness (search seeds, exploration draws) changed and everything else the same; no option changes those, and no A/A test has been run <Badge text="planned" variant="caution" />. So the gates' real false-pass rates are not measured.

## 5. The layered gates

| tier | for | method | state |
|---|---|---|---|
| combat panel | combat search and its leaf | the same fights for every player (encounter, deck, seed), so CRN; win rate and HP lost per win | built: `panel` ([Simulator: reference §4](/en/arch/simulator/reference/#sec-4)); results in [The SL planner: budget §5](/en/arch/sl-planner/budget/#sec-5) and [NoSL PIMC: budget §6](/en/arch/pimc/budget/#sec-6) |
| act-level | macro decisions | acts started from a pool of snapshots; act clear rate and HP at the act's end | planned <Badge text="planned" variant="caution" /> |
| whole-run SPRT, `gate` | a change judged sequentially, such as a cheaper budget or a removed hand-written part | paired by seed on a promotion set; SPRT on floors or on wins | built ([§6](#sec-6)) |
| whole-run wins bound | a new net, V or combat | `learn/gauntlet.py --against` on whole runs: paired by seed on `promotion-1-300` at medium; lower bound on wins | in use ([§10](#sec-10)) |

The panel costs seconds to minutes a fight; a whole-run gate costs hours of compute. Version gates use whole runs: a panel can show a search is stronger in a fight, not that a run goes further ([Combat brain §5](/en/arch/combat-brain/#sec-5)).

## 6. The whole-run gate

```text
gate --champion eval/CHARACTER/champions/ENV.json --records A[,B…] [the challenger's run options]
     [--metric floor|win] [--delta D] [--alpha A] [--beta B] [--not-worse] [--promote PATH] [--env ENV]
```

(`SimWorker/Commands/EvalCommands.cs:63-126`, `Harness/Gate.cs`)

| option | default | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| `--champion FILE` | required | the champion file to beat; its `report` is the champion's side |
| `--records A[,B…]` | required | the challenger's run record files, comma-separated |
| `--metric` | `floor` | `floor` or `win` ([§3](#sec-3)) |
| `--delta D` | 1.5 for `floor`, 0.1 for `win` (`:97`) | the difference the SPRT tells from none |
| `--alpha A` | 0.05 | the chance to pass a challenger no better (with `--not-worse`: worse by δ) |
| `--beta B` | 0.05 | the chance to fail one better by δ (with `--not-worse`: no worse) |
| `--not-worse` | off | the non-inferiority test ([§7](#sec-7)) |
| `--promote PATH` | none | on a pass, writes the challenger's report and makes it the champion ([§8](#sec-8)) |
| `--env ENV` | none | the challenger's environment, refused if it is not the file's |
| the challenger's run options | | `--policy`, `--macro`, the budget, `--workers`, `--value`, `--afterstates`, `--combat-net`, `--macro-explore`, `--combat-explore`, `--explore` ([Simulator: reference §3](/en/arch/simulator/reference/#sec-3)); there is no `--champion FILE` for the challenger, which is named in full by its options |

**The two sides.** The champion's side is its report's runs. The challenger's side is its records, and every record must have been played by the options given: the name the options make must be each record's `config.champion` (`:94-95`). The pairs are the champion report's runs whose seed the challenger played, in the champion report's order, which is the seed set's (`Gate.Pairs`, `Harness/Gate.cs:46-60`). A time-boxed challenger that finished only the head of the set is gated on the pairs it has.

**Refused**, as a command-line mistake (exit 2):

- exploring options (`--macro-explore`, `--combat-explore` or `--explore` above 0; the message names only the first two, `:93`): exploration is for self-play ([Learning: the loop §9](/en/arch/learning/loop/#sec-9));
- a record played by another champion name than the options', or records of more than one;
- records with `auto_win` or `gauntlet` in their config: some of their combats were never played;
- a record played in another game version, environment or rung than the champion's report, or as another character or at another ascension; so a NoSL challenger against today's NoSL file, which names an SL report, is refused;
- a seed run twice; `--env` that is not the file's.

**Not checked:** the commit. A challenger built at another commit pairs with the champion's report all the same, which is why the champion is re-baselined when the code changes how it plays ([§9](#sec-9)). Nor is the champion report's own void checked, and challenger runs whose seed is not in the champion's report are silently left out.

**Verdicts.** `pass`, `fail`, `open` (the pairs ran out first), or `void`: the paired challenger runs break a must-be-zero rule, whatever the SPRT said (`Harness/Gate.cs:67-70, 106`).

**Output and exit code.** Three lines, then a fourth on promotion:

```text
champion: NAME (eval/silent/reports/REPORT.json)
challenger: NAME, 200 runs
gate pass: floor, delta 1.5, after 136 of 200 pairs; LLR 2.99 in [-2.94, 2.94]; floor difference +1.78; wins only by the challenger 0, only by the champion 0
promoted: eval/silent/champions/nosl.json now names NAME
```

(the shape of a real floor gate's output, the `Summary` form, `Harness/Gate.cs:37-40`, with its names and paths left out). The exit code is 0 on a pass, promoted or not; 1 on fail, open or void; 2 on a command-line mistake, a refused promotion, or a file that cannot be read (`SimWorker/Commands/Cli.cs:50-61`).

**Time boxes.** Evaluations run in time boxes: the job gets a deadline and more seeds than fit, seeds are dealt from the front of the file, and what finished is the result (`.claude/skills/sl-selfplay/SKILL.md:108-112`). Runs that die early finish first, so a set cut short leans towards short runs. The two sides of a comparison of two time-boxed jobs are therefore given the same machines and the same box, so they are cut alike.

## 7. Not worse

A change meant to be cheaper rather than stronger (searching less, the same play otherwise) is promoted when it is **not worse**. `--not-worse` shifts both hypotheses down by δ: H0, the challenger is worse by δ ($\mu = -\delta$); H1, it is not worse ($\mu = 0$). The code adds δ to every difference and runs the usual test (`Harness/Gate.cs:72-73, 90`). For `win`, H0 is a share of 0.5 − δ of the discordant pairs, H1 an even share. The gate's line and its stored result say which test it was (`not worse` in the line, `not_worse` in the champion file).

The project uses it for one more case: a challenger that only removes a hand-written part passes on a tie, since the simpler brain is preferred when it is not worse. The SL planner's medium effort, its default budget, passed such a gate after 33 pairs ([The SL planner: budget §1](/en/arch/sl-planner/budget/#sec-1)).

## 8. Promotion

On a pass, `--promote PATH` (`SimWorker/Commands/EvalCommands.cs:113-123`):

1. makes the challenger's report from its records on the champion's seed set (`eval/seeds/SET.txt`), dated today; refuses a partial one, since the new report faces the next challengers and must cover the whole set;
2. makes the champion file's options: the challenger's options for the file's environment, plus its character and ascension. Each weights file (`value`, `combat-net`) must be in the character's weights folder, `weights/CHARACTER/`, where a name always means the same file; a promotion with a file elsewhere is refused before anything is written (`SimWorker/Brains/ChampionOptions.cs:116-126`, [Learning: reference §6](/en/arch/learning/reference/#sec-6));
3. writes `PATH.json` and `PATH.md`, the Markdown with an "Against" section on the champion's report;
4. rewrites the champion file: the environment, the challenger's name and options, the new report, `since` the report's date, and `gate` the result ([Champions and baselines §1](/en/arch/evaluation/champions/#sec-1)).

The SPRT can pass early, and a promotion needs the whole set. So a challenger can be played in chunks and gated after each: stop at a fail, and after a pass play the rest of the set and gate again with `--promote`.
## 9. Re-baselining

The champion's report stands for the champion only while the code plays it as it did. A change that makes it play otherwise (a fix in the world builder, the search or L0) makes the report stale, and a gate against it would credit the challenger with the change; the gate does not check the commit ([§6](#sec-6)). The champion then plays the whole seed set again on the new code (`run --champion FILE`), and its champion file is edited to name the new report. That is not a promotion: the champion, its date and its gate stay.

An earlier NoSL champion shows the size of the effect, computed from its reports on `promotion-1`. After a round of fixes to the search, L0 and the NoSL worlds, its mean floor went from 21.88 to 22.70, +0.81 ± 0.89 floors paired by seed (95%), with 131 seeds to the same floor, 42 higher and 27 lower. After a later pair of small fixes, all 200 seeds reached the same floor.
## 10. The wins gate

The wins gate judges a new net on whole SL runs by wins, with `learn/gauntlet.py`'s paired lower bound, not with `gate` ([Evaluation §4](/en/arch/evaluation/#sec-4)).

```text
uv run python gauntlet.py --records R1,R2 [--logs L1,L2] [--against C1,C2]
```

(`learn/gauntlet.py:164-183`, run from `learn/`.) It prints one JSON object. Its report:

| field | what |
|---|---|
| `gauntlet` | false for whole runs; records of another kind are never mixed with them, nor paired (`:42-48`, `:180-181`) |
| `runs`, `won`, `floor`, `floor_se` | the finished runs, the runs won, the mean floor and its standard error |
| `boss` | per act `"0"`, `"1"`, `"2"`: `{reached, beaten, rate}`, counting every boss combat of the act and those that did not end in a death |
| `elites_per_run`, `elite_deaths` | elite combats per run, and the elite combats that ended in a death |
| `died_in` | per act, the lost runs by where they died: the room of the last combat if it ended in a death on the run's floor, else `outside combat` (a combat with no end counts as outside, [Metrics, seeds and reports §8](/en/arch/evaluation/metrics/#sec-8)) |
| `rest`, `monster_or_rest`, `shop` | with `--logs`: the rest sites' choices by the HP share they were made at (0–40%, 40–70%, 70–90%, 90–100%), the map's choices between a monster and a rest site by the same bands, and what the shops sold (relic, card, potion or other, told by what the next decision's run gained after a `buy:`; `remove`, `leave`); only logged runs that have a record (`:107-133`) |
| `against` | with `--against`, the pairing below |

**The rule.** `--against` names the champion's records. The pairs are the seeds both sides finished. For each, $d_i$ is the challenger's outcome less the champion's: 1 or 0 per run for wins, the floor for floors. With $n$ pairs, $\bar d$ their mean and $\mathrm{SE} = \mathrm{sd}(d)/\sqrt n$ (the sample SD, $n-1$),

$$
\text{lower} = \bar d - 1.645\,\mathrm{SE}, \qquad \text{promote if lower} > 0,
$$

the 95% one-sided lower bound of the mean difference (1.645 is the normal distribution's 95% quantile). `against` holds `pairs`, `won`, `champion_won`, `wins_diff`, `wins_diff_se`, `wins_lower_95`, `floor_diff`, `floor_diff_se` and `promote`, decided on the wins (`learn/gauntlet.py:136-161`). With one pair the SE is undefined and nothing is promoted; with every pair tied the bound is 0 and nothing is promoted.

**What it takes to pass.** In terms of the discordant seeds ([§4](#sec-4)), $\bar d = (k - m)/n$, and the bound is above 0 roughly when $k - m > 1.645\sqrt{k + m}$. Computed exactly with the rule, at 300 pairs:

| seeds won only by the champion, $m$ | 0 | 1 | 5 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fewest won only by the challenger, $k$ | 3 | 6 | 12 | 19 | 32 | 45 | 57 | 80 |
| the win rate it adds | +1.0 pp | +1.7 pp | +2.3 pp | +3.0 pp | +4.0 pp | +5.0 pp | +5.7 pp | +6.7 pp |

The margin needed grows with the champion's own wins. With few discordant seeds the normal bound is lenient: 3 seeds won by the challenger alone and none by the champion pass, where an exact McNemar (sign) test gives a one-sided p of 1/8.

**What it does not check.** `gauntlet.py` drops every record with a `failure` before anything (`:44`), so it has no void rule; it has no minimum of pairs, and does not compare the two sides' configs, champions, environments, commits or exploration. The procedure keeps those: the challenger plays `promotion-1-300` at medium with the champion's planner, `--afterstates clone` and no `--explore`, in a time box, and the champion plays in the same box any of those seeds it has no runs on (`.claude/skills/sl-selfplay/SKILL.md:87-100`). Both also play the same seeds at the low budget: that comparison is for reference and decides nothing.

**At ascension 10** the gate plays 200 seeds at medium and 200 at the low budget, the first 200 of `promotion-1-300`, with the reports on `promotion-1-200`. A V and a combat net are judged apart, in the four pairings of old and new. A net is judged by its paired wins, with its floors listed beside them, and fewer floors do not block a net that wins more: at A10, strength late in a run is paid for with early floors. The first A10 gate went by floors, and is on [Champions and baselines §2](/en/arch/evaluation/champions/#sec-2).

**Promotion** is by hand: the script writes nothing. A net that passes (of two that pass, the one with more wins) replaces its net in both champion files, the ledger records it, and a `report` of its runs at medium goes on the leaderboard.

**On committed reports** (computed here; no such gate ran), with A, B and C of [§2](#sec-2) on `promotion-1-300`: C against A, 300 pairs, 23 won against 1, +0.073 ± 0.016 wins, lower bound +0.047, so it would pass (the floor: +2.20 ± 0.62). B against A would pass too, 13 against 1, lower bound +0.020, while its floor is −0.26 ± 0.70: floors and wins can disagree. C against B, 21 against 11 with 2 seeds won by both, passes by +0.0024.

## 11. Tests

- `Harness/GateTests` (`src/Sts2Ai.Tests`), 10 tests: a clear gain passes, at exactly `MinPairs`, with mean 5; pairs only the champion's character and ascension; no gain fails; a not-worse gate passes no loss, fails a loss of 5, and says so in its line; no verdict before `MinPairs`; tied pairs do not count towards it (one run in ten +15 floors passes, after more than 100 pairs); a failed challenger run voids; the win gate counts only one-sided wins, and an unknown metric throws; the pairs follow the champion's order; another environment, a seed twice and two champion names are refused. The figure's port of `Gate.Sprt` gives the same verdicts on the same cases.
- `learn/tests/test_gauntlet.py` covers the report (bosses, elites and deaths), rest sites by HP and shop purchases, the map's monster-or-rest choices by HP, and the rule: whole runs pair on wins and floors and promote on wins (4 pairs, 3 against 0, lower bound 0.75 − 1.645 · 0.25), and records of different kinds are never paired.
- Nothing tests the `gate` command itself or its promotion.
