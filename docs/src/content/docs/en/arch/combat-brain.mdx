The combat brain answers two kinds of question during a fight: (a) on the player's turn, what to do next (a card and its target, a potion, or end turn); (b) the prompts that come up during play (discard, exhaust, choose, …). It runs in the decision process; [Harness](/en/arch/harness/) calls it with a set of worlds and a budget. The two environments pose different problems, so they get different algorithms behind one interface: NoSL averages over sampled worlds ([§2](#sec-2)), SL plans on one exact world ([§3](#sec-3)). Harness picks the brain by environment ([Environments §5](/en/arch/environments/#sec-5)).

<Aside title="Status">

v0 exists (`src/Sts2Ai.Brain/CombatBrain.cs`, `LeafValue.cs`): PIMC over K worlds with a search over this turn ([§2](#sec-2)), a hand-made leaf value and a learned one, the combat net, whose first generation c2 the NoSL champion plays since 2026-10-27 ([§4](#sec-4), [§7.2](#sec-7.2)), a fixed rule for card prompts (`p0`, [§2](#sec-2), rule 7) and the fixed combat panel with its results ([§7](#sec-7)). The SL planner (`SlPlanner.cs`, 2026-10-28) plans the whole combat on SL's one world and follows its plan ([§3](#sec-3)). Not built yet: card prompts as search branches, PIMC's search past this turn, CI-based allocation and `Facts`. The placeholders stay, for bench and as the panel's baselines: `PlayPolicy` (plays a random playable card) and `RandomPolicy` (uniform over the legal actions).

</Aside>

## 1. Problem shape

- A turn is a sequence of actions that ends with `end_turn`. Uncertainty inside a turn comes from draws and from random generation. The enemy turn is deterministic once the monsters' moves are fixed.
- Branching: a hand of 5–10 cards, targeted cards times the number of enemies, plus potions; a sequence is 3–8 actions long. That gives up to 10⁴ sequences per turn, with many **transpositions**: different action orders that reach the same state (A then B gives the same state as B then A).
- Horizon: a combat lasts 3–15 turns. Searching only one turn is greedy: it sees nothing past the turn. Searching the whole combat is expensive, because in NoSL it crosses many chance nodes (points where the game resolves something random, so the search has to branch over the outcomes). So the search is cut at the end of the turn, and the state there is scored by a leaf value in P(win run) units ([§4](#sec-4)).
- Cost ([Simulator §4](/en/arch/simulator/#sec-4)): a restore takes 0.28 ms plus 0.073 ms per replayed action; a branch takes about 2.8 ms (median), about 330 per second per core; 8 workers give about 6.8 times that.

## 2. NoSL: PIMC

In NoSL the AI does not know the hidden state (draw order, monster moves). The design handles this with **PIMC** (perfect-information Monte Carlo): draw K determinized worlds, i.e. complete hidden states that agree with the public record; search each one as an ordinary game with nothing hidden; and average the results. The answer is an expectation over the worlds.

PIMC has one known weakness, **strategy fusion**: inside one world the search knows that world's future, so if it may choose a different line in each world, the plan acts as if it already knew which world is the real one. The alternative that avoids this is **ISMCTS** (information-set Monte Carlo tree search). It builds one search tree over information sets (groups of states the player cannot tell apart) and samples a different world on each pass, so no choice in the tree can depend on hidden information. The design starts with PIMC and keeps fusion small: it aggregates over the first action only and re-plans after every action (step 3). ISMCTS is held in reserve in case fusion is measured to be a problem (open question 2, [§8](#sec-8)).

<Steps>

1. **Worlds.** Harness supplies K worlds: start with 8, and add more when the top two candidates are within their confidence intervals (v0: a fixed K, 8 by default). Each world has a different draw order, different random streams and different monster moves, all consistent with the public record.
2. **Search inside the turn.** Depth-first search (DFS: follow one action sequence to its end, then back up and try the next) over action sequences. At every node of the search, ending the turn is one of the options, an idea from bottled_ai, a prior-art project this design learned from. Within a world, states are deduplicated by the observation's hash (of its JSON) together with the world's hidden state (`ICombatWorld.HiddenKey`: the draw pile's order, the position of every random stream, each monster's queued move), so each transposition is expanded once, and two orders that look the same but drew or rolled differently stay apart: keyed by the observation alone, the first of them to be searched gave its value to the other. How often that happened was measured with a probe that kept both keys (2026-10-27): on Silent's starter deck (about 990 decisions, 376k states) and on a deck of random-effect cards (`RICOCHET`, `BOUNCING_FLASK`, `VOLLEY`, `SEEKER_STRIKE`, `JACKPOT`, `CINDER`; 250 act 0 decisions, 81k states, 8.6k repeats), no repeated observation had another hidden state, so no decision changed. In a NoSL world the hidden state is the world's own sample, drawn from the observation and the search seed, so the key carries nothing of the real game's. The order of the turn's plays is left out of the key, so the same plays in another order still meet. A state that the budget cut short is not kept for its transpositions, since its value is only a lower bound.
3. **Aggregate by first action.** In each world, take the best value reachable after the first action; then take the mean over the K worlds. Never take the max across worlds: that is strategy fusion. Execute the first action only, observe again, and plan again.
   - Residual fusion: after the first action, each world's plan is still clairvoyant, so lines like "play X, then see what's drawn" are overvalued. If this is measured to be a problem, switch to ISMCTS.
4. **Enemy turn.** The enemy turn is simulated for real, with the world's sampled monster moves. The leaf is taken at the start of the next player turn, so the value sees the state after the enemies have acted. A line also ends when the combat does.
5. **More turns** (planned) when the budget allows: expand the top first actions one more turn, with a cheap policy or with beam search for turn 2 (beam search: at each step keep only the B best states and expand only those).

</Steps>

Two more rules:

6. Potions are ordinary actions. Their timing is not hard-coded.
7. Prompts: inside search, the simulator's selector (the code that answers card prompts) is driven by the search, so each option is a branch. A lone prompt in the real game is handled by evaluating each option with the same search (this needs `ChoiceContext`, [Protocol §4](/en/arch/protocol/#sec-4)). v0 does neither yet. Every card prompt in a combat, in the real combat and inside a world, goes to `PromptBrain` (`p0`, in Brain), a fixed rule by what happens to the picked cards:
   - Picks that leave the hand this turn (a discard, an exhaust, a card put on top of the draw pile) are the least useful cards, Sly cards first in a discard, since discarding plays them. Past its minimum such a prompt takes only the cards better gone: unplayable ones, and Sly ones in a discard.
   - A prompt that makes a card free takes the dearest one.
   - Any other prompt adds or improves cards (a card into the hand, a copy, an upgrade), so it takes the most useful ones, as many as it may.

   A card's use is its numbers summed (damage, block, poison, draw, energy, Weak and Vulnerable, upgrades) plus a flat part; an unplayable card has none. `p0` reads the prompt alone and never draws at random, so a world answers as the run will.

### 2.1 Where the randomness is, and what PIMC estimates

**Monster moves.** Each monster picks its moves with a move state machine in the game's code. Three kinds of rule occur, most monsters mixing them:

- a fixed cycle (attack, defend, attack, …; or charge, then the big hit), deterministic given the moves so far;
- a weighted roll with constraints, e.g. 60% attack and 40% buff, but never the same move three times in a row, or one move always followed by another;
- a condition, e.g. another move set below half HP, or enemies taking turns.

The rolls draw from the monster's own random stream, which the run's seed fixes: the same seed gives the same moves, which is what SL may exploit. The player never sees the stream, so in NoSL a roll is unknown until its intent shows. This round's move is always known, since its intent shows. A player learns a monster quickly because the constraints leave the rolls little room; what stays unknown is exactly the rolls. The combat net shows the same picture: it names the next round's intent type 57.7% of the time, against 28.9% for repeating the current one, and its misses fall mostly on the rolls.

**What the worlds sample.** The hidden state $s$ of a combat is the draw pile's order, the position of every random stream and, for each monster, which of the moves showing its intent it has queued. The public record $h$ is the observation plus the intents shown each round. A world is one sample of $s$ that agrees with $h$. Later moves are not sampled separately: the world's own state machine makes them, from the log rebuilt from $h$ ([Simulator §3](/en/arch/simulator/#sec-3)) and the world's random stream. All of it, cards, rolls and the enemy turn, is played by the real game code in the worker; no net simulates anything. The net only values the leaves.

**The estimate.** For a first action $a$, PIMC computes

$$
\hat Q(a) = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \max_{\pi} V\big(\mathrm{leaf}(s_k, a, \pi)\big), \qquad s_k \sim \tilde P(s \mid h)
$$

where $\pi$ ranges over the rest of the turn, $\mathrm{leaf}$ is the state at the start of the next player turn ([§2](#sec-2), step 4) and $V$ the leaf value ([§4](#sec-4)). It plays $\arg\max_a \hat Q(a)$. Three properties follow:

- **Noise.** $\hat Q(a)$ is a mean of $K$ samples, so its standard error is $\sigma_a / \sqrt{K}$. Every first action is scored on the same $K$ worlds (common random numbers), so the noise of the difference between two actions is much smaller than either one's: the world's luck cancels.
- **Bias up: strategy fusion.** The quantity estimated is $\mathbb{E}_s[\max_\pi V]$. The player must fix the rest of the turn before seeing $s$, which is worth $\max_\pi \mathbb{E}_s[V]$. The mean of maxima is never below the maximum of means (Jensen), so PIMC overvalues lines whose later choices depend on what is drawn or rolled. Re-planning after every action keeps the gap to what is still hidden after the first action (open question 2, [§8](#sec-8)).
- **Bias from the sampler.** The worlds should follow the posterior $P(s \mid h)$; they follow the sampler's $\tilde P(s \mid h)$. It shuffles the draw pile uniformly, which is exact, but picks a monster's queued move uniformly among the moves that show its intent, not by the roll's weights, and keeps no posterior over hidden counters ([Simulator §6](/en/arch/simulator/#sec-6)). A more exact sampler lowers this bias without changing the search.

## 3. SL: deterministic planning

In SL the combat brain gets one exact world, the node clone. The clone holds every random stream, so the same action sequence always gives the same outcome. There is no hidden state and no chance node, so the combat is a deterministic planning problem. That is a different problem from NoSL's, and it gets its own algorithm, the **SL planner** (`SlPlanner`, built): a second implementation of `ICombatBrain` ([§6](#sec-6)).

**What optimal means.** With one world, taking the best line is correct: strategy fusion needs several worlds to fuse, so knowing the future is not an error here. A combat has finitely many lines, so a best line exists and exhaustive search finds it. It is best with respect to the value at the end of the combat (or at the node exit): once the search reaches the end, the fight itself holds no estimate, and the only error left is in the value of the end state (HP, potions, max HP, gold, …).

**How hard it is.** In general, with hand, energy and turns unbounded, the problem is NP-hard: choosing a turn's cards under an energy limit already contains a knapsack problem. The real instances are bounded (a hand of at most 10 cards, 3–15 turns) but large: up to 10⁴ sequences per turn ([§1](#sec-1)), over many turns. So the planner aims at the optimum and proves it when it can, but does not need it.

The planner is a beam over turns, with each turn searched in full:

<Steps>

1. **Search a turn in full.** From one state, a depth-first search over the turn's actions finds every distinct state where the turn ends. Ending the turn is an option at every step, and the enemies' turn after it is played for real, so a turn ends at the next turn's first decision; a line also ends where the combat does. Card prompts go to `p0`, as in the run ([§2](#sec-2), rule 7). The turn's nodes are split over its first actions: the k-th of m may search until k/m of them are used, so what an earlier action leaves goes to the later ones, and the nodes are not all spent under the first card.
2. **Key by full state.** Inside a turn, transpositions are merged by the observation and the world's full hidden state (`HiddenState.Full`): the draw pile's order, the position of every random stream, each monster's queued move and its moves so far, and what the combat's history holds of this turn, as a multiset, so that the same plays in another order still meet. Two orders that reach the same observation with the streams in different states (after a reshuffle, a random target or a generated card) draw different futures: that is the RNG manipulation SL allows, and the search sees it for free. Only one turn's transpositions are merged, so every state the planner keeps is the end of a concrete line, and the plan replays exactly.
3. **Beam over turns.** Each state where a turn ends is scored by the leaf ([§4](#sec-4)), which reads its observation only. Of all the kept states' turn ends, the best B (`Budget.Beam`, `--beam`, 8 by default), distinct by key, are the next turn's states. Each kept state's turn is searched with the budget's nodes per world (`--nodes`, 300 by default; `--elite-x` and `--boss-x` multiply them). The beam goes on until no state is left, or for at most 40 turns.
4. **Bound.** A line that ends the combat is scored by the leaf at its end: a win by the HP and potions left, a loss 0. The best such line prunes every state whose bound, a win with the HP and the potions it has, cannot beat it. The bound assumes that the fight neither heals nor gives potions, which a few cards, potions and relics break; the prune can then drop a line that would have been better.
5. **Dive first.** Before the full beam, a beam of one, a greedy line by the leaf, runs to the end of the combat. Its line, a win in most fights, is the floor that prunes the full beam from its first turn on. When it reaches the decision's bound (a win that loses no HP and no potion) nothing can beat it, and the full beam is skipped.
6. **Pick.** The best line that ends the combat, unless the beam stopped at its turn cap at a state the leaf values higher. A plan is **proved** when its line reaches the decision's bound, or when the full beam left nothing out: no turn's search ran out of nodes or found more states than it kept, no state was dropped from the beam, and every line ended or was pruned. Every loss is worth 0, so among losing lines the one that died from the turn start the leaf valued highest is the pick. A losing plan that is not proved is played for its first turn only: the next turn is planned again, from where a beam reaches deeper. A proved loss is played to the end, since no line does better.
7. **Plan once.** The world is exact, so the plan is computed once and played as computed. The plan is the line's actions with the observation hash each one expects, and Harness's route plays it step by step with `TurnPlan` ([§5](#sec-5)). A step whose observation differs means the clone differs from the real combat: the route plans again and marks the decision `replanned` in the decision log ([Harness §6](/en/arch/harness/#sec-6)), and the run's line counts the replans. Relic state that the save drops was one source, until the checkpoint carried it ([Simulator §7](/en/arch/simulator/#sec-7), [§7.2](#sec-7.2)).
8. **The value at the end.** The leaf ends the line where the combat ends: a won combat is priced by its HP share and potions at `LeafValue`'s prices, the same function NoSL's search ends its turns with. The design aims at V of the end state, the same V as in NoSL and in the macro brain ([§4](#sec-4)); that, and searching on to the node exit (open question 1 in [Environments §10](/en/arch/environments/#sec-10)), are not built.
9. **Workers.** The turns' searches are independent, so each turn of the beam is one request per kept state (`sl_turn`, [Simulator §8](/en/arch/simulator/#sec-8)): the worker loads the node's clone, plays the state's path and searches its turn. The beam splits the work by action prefix, and each request carries the best whole line so far as the floor of its pruning. Replies merge in request order, so a plan is the same on any number of workers and in one process.
10. **Seal.** The clone stops at the node exit (`AtHorizon`), and the SL world refuses to step past the end of its combat, so search never leaves the room.

</Steps>

Not built: improving the beam's line by branch-and-bound until a budget runs out (wider beams in turn, as an anytime search), card prompts as branches, and V pricing the end state.

PIMC on the one world (`--policy pimc --env SL`) stays, as the planner's baseline: it searches this turn only, and plays no better than NoSL on the panel ([§7.1](#sec-7.1)), since knowing the future helps only a search that looks past the turn.

## 4. Leaf value

The leaf value scores a state where the search stops. It uses one scale: the estimated probability of winning the run, P(win run) ([Learning](/en/arch/learning/)).

- The input is the observation only, in both environments, so it cannot leak hidden state.
- It is one function for both environments, shared with the macro brain ([Learning §1](/en/arch/learning/#sec-1)). NoSL uses it at the end of the turn; SL mostly at the end of the combat ([§3](#sec-3)).
- v0 (built, `LeafValue`) projects the rest of the fight from the observation:
  - A lost combat is 0. A won combat scores `WinBase + PerHp × (HP / max HP)`, plus `PerPotion` for each potion still held.
  - Mid-combat, each enemy alive gets an effective HP (HP + block − `PoisonShare` × the poison's remaining damage n + (n−1) + … + 1) and a damage rate (its shown attack this turn, damage × hits, at least `MinIncoming`). The player deals `Dps` + 2 × Strength per turn and kills the enemies one after another, the highest rate per HP first. That is Smith's rule, the order that minimizes the total of (rate × time until dead). Each enemy hits until it dies; `Unblocked` of the damage beyond the player's current block is taken off the HP, and the HP left is scored like a win.
  - So damage is worth more on an enemy that hits hard and dies soon, overkill is worth nothing, and a mid-combat state never beats the win it could reach at the same HP.
  - A projected death (HP share below 0) decays toward 0 exponentially, with the same slope at 0, so it stays above a loss and still orders lines. A flat floor made every losing line look the same, and the brain then ended its turns early.
  - The weights are hand-set (`LeafWeights`: WinBase 0.2, PerHp 0.6, PerPotion 0.02, Dps 10, Unblocked 0.5, MinIncoming 3, PoisonShare 0.8). Blind spots: powers other than poison on enemies and Strength on the player, the draw pile, and intents after this turn.
  - `--leaf FILE` gives the brain other weights, in `run`, `brain` and `panel`, and passes them on to the workers (`serve --leaf`). `weights --leaf` prints the default ones as such a file, named `leaf0`; other weights show in the champion's name (`combat pimc NAME`). `learn/spsa.py --param leaf` tunes them ([Learning §2](/en/arch/learning/#sec-2)). `--combat-net FILE` gives the brain the learned leaf below instead, in the same commands, passed on as `serve --combat-net`; its name shows the same way.
- Next: hand-made features times linear weights. The feature list is seeded from bottled_ai's 42 features (player HP, block, enemy HP, powers, draw-pile quality, turns to kill, …). bottled_ai compares them in lexicographic order (the first feature decides, the next one only breaks ties); that order becomes linear weights spaced far apart, which are then tuned by SPSA (simultaneous perturbation stochastic approximation: tunes all weights at once from noisy results by perturbing them together). It must value block, overkill and lethal next turn without saturating.
- The learned leaf (built, `CombatNet`, [Learning §1](/en/arch/learning/#sec-1)): a net on the encoded observation and the enemies' intents in the rounds so far. Its head predicts the combat's outcome distribution: a death class plus buckets of the HP left at the end (an idea from r33hab, another prior-art project this design learned from, and from ytcs). For now the leaf prices the outcomes at `LeafValue`'s prices for a won combat (WinBase, PerHp, PerPotion). Later the macro V prices each outcome once per combat, and the leaf is the priced mean ([Learning §3](/en/arch/learning/#sec-3)), so the net is not retrained for a new V. A combat already over is valued as `LeafValue` values it. Two training targets are compared through the gate ([Learning §1](/en/arch/learning/#sec-1)); the first net trains on MC targets.
- Both leaves are an `ICombatLeaf` ([§6](#sec-6)): a state, its status and the combat's record of enemy moves (`MoveRecord`). Harness hands the record to the combat brain with each decision, and `PooledPimc` sends it to the workers in each request ([Simulator §8](/en/arch/simulator/#sec-8)). Every leaf of a decision's search follows the same record: the leaves are the next round's starts, and the record holds the rounds up to the decision's.
- The mid-combat leaf (at turn end) and the post-combat leaf (combat won, before rewards) are the same function; in the post-combat case the combat part is empty.

## 5. Budget and pruning

The levers below keep the search inside its budget. The one that decides when to stop is **CI-based allocation**: each first action's value is a mean over worlds, with a confidence interval (CI), a range that very likely contains the true mean. Once the two best first actions are clearly apart, more samples will not change the choice, so the search stops; otherwise it spends more worlds on those two.

| lever | effect | v0 |
|---|---|---|
| canonical obs-hash dedup | each distinct observation is expanded once per world | yes |
| move ordering + early stop | expand high-prior actions first (a heuristic first, a policy net later) | end turn first, then cards in hand order, then potions; stop when the share is spent |
| CI-based allocation over first actions | stop when the top two are separated; otherwise give them more worlds | no: every first action gets every world |
| trivial decisions | one legal action, or all sequences equivalent → no search | one legal action |
| restore cost | DFS order reuses prefixes and restores only on backtrack; deep copy later ([Simulator §4](/en/arch/simulator/#sec-4)) | yes |
| sequential halving at the root | instead of the even split: ⌈log₂ m⌉ rounds; each round splits every world's nodes evenly over the first actions left, then drops the worse half by their mean over the same worlds (CRN) | built (`--halving`), off by default. Workers keep nothing between requests, so each round searches the actions left again from the root with all the nodes they have had so far: up to about 1.5 times the nodes of resuming (1.25 at 4 first actions, 1.4 at 8, 1.5 at 16) |
| following the plan | the rest of a turn that every world planned the same is played without searching again, a step at a time, while the combat shows the observation every world expected before it (hashes on the lines); a draw, a random result or a disagreement between worlds sends the decision back to search | built (`--follow`), off by default. On the first 6 act 1 panel combats, 66 of 142 decisions were played without a search, every action the same, in 27% less time. In whole runs of the champion on 16 train seeds, 31% less time per run. Its not-worse gate ([Evaluation §5](/en/arch/evaluation/#sec-5)) was open on all 200 seeds: −0.72 ± 0.92 floors, 126 seeds to the same floor, so it is not promoted |
| budget by combat | an elite's or a boss's decisions get a multiple of the nodes per world (`--elite-x`, `--boss-x`, by the observation's room type) | built, × 1 by default. Boss × 2 failed its gate on seeds 0–99 (2026-10-28): 90 of the 100 pairs tied and the mean difference was −0.39 ± 0.32 floors; 43 runs passed the act 1 boss against the champion's 45, and runs took 15% longer. Not adopted; × 4 untried |
| K and nodes per world | SPSA parameters | no: planned; 8 × 300 for every combat |
| SL planner: beam and nodes per state | B states kept per turn, each turn searched from each with the nodes per world; a greedy dive first, whose line prunes the beam ([§3](#sec-3)) | built: `--beam 8 --nodes 300` by default. On 11 act 1 fights (5 weak + regular, 6 elites, the same 8 won by every setting), beam 4 kept less HP than beam 8 (219 against 238 over the 8). Beam 8 with 150 nodes and beam 16 with 150 differed from beam 8 by up to 17 HP on three or four fights, either way, for about the same total (229 and 232). Beam 16 × 300 kept 16 more HP over the 5 hallway fights, all of it in one fight, in about 1.7 times the time |

v0's budget is `Budget(Worlds, NodesPerWorld)`, 8 × 300 by default; a node is one executed action. In each world the nodes are split evenly over the first actions, so each gets a fair look. Inside a subtree, once its share is spent, the search stops expanding and the state there is scored as ending the turn, which is the option tried first. On one core this costs about 0.55–0.75 s per action in NoSL and 0.13–0.19 s in SL (one world). In NoSL the worlds are independent, so the worker pool searches one world per worker ([Simulator §8](/en/arch/simulator/#sec-8)): with 8 workers an action takes about 70 ms, with the same result.

**The newer levers.** Sequential halving (SH) is the root selection of Gumbel AlphaZero when the prior is uniform; assist mode, whose time is open-ended, uses its anytime variant. SH and CI-based allocation share the per-world means: SH decides which first action gets the nodes, CI when to stop. The boss multiplier has the strongest evidence on STS: in spire-mentor, raising the boss budget from 1,000 to 4,000 lifted the act 1 clear rate from 21.5% to over 65%, and our runs end mostly at the act 1 boss or in act 2 ([§7.2](#sec-7.2)). K is tuned rather than grown: LoCM (a card-game study) saturates beyond 32 worlds, and ytcs gained 0.018 from K = 64.

Rough numbers: 1–2 s per decision times 8 workers gives about 5,000 branches. With K=16 that is about 300 branches per world, enough for a deduplicated enumeration inside one turn.

## 6. Interface

```csharp
public sealed record Budget(int Worlds = 8, int NodesPerWorld = 300, double Elite = 1, double Boss = 1, bool Halving = false,
    bool Follow = false, int Beam = 0);   // Beam: the SL planner's states per turn; PIMC leaves it 0

public interface ICombatBrain
{
    // Player's turn: search the worlds, return the first action and the rest of the plan.
    // history: the combat's record of enemy moves so far (MoveRecord), for the leaves.
    CombatDecisionResult Decide(CombatObservation observation, IReadOnlyList<CombatAction> legal,
                                IWorldSource worlds, Budget budget, ulong seed,
                                IReadOnlyList<EnemyTurns>? history = null);
}

public interface ICombatLeaf
{
    // A search leaf: the state after the turn ends, or after the combat does.
    double Value(CombatStatus status, CombatObservation obs, IReadOnlyList<EnemyTurns> history);
}

public sealed record CombatDecisionResult(
    CombatAction First,                    // one of legal
    IReadOnlyList<CombatAction> Plan,      // rest of this turn in the first world (narration, highlight)
    IReadOnlyList<Candidate> Candidates);  // every first action, best first
```

`PimcBrain` implements it for NoSL; `SlPlanner` ([§3](#sec-3)) is the second implementation, for SL, and Harness picks one by environment. `SlPlanner.Plan` also returns the plan's value, how it ends, its nodes and turns, and whether it is proved. In a run the planner searches in the worker pool through `PooledPlanner`, PIMC through `PooledPimc` ([Simulator §8](/en/arch/simulator/#sec-8)). Planned: `Choose(request, worlds, budget)` for a prompt during play (chosen canonical indexes plus a value per option), and `Facts` on the result (expected HP loss, turns to kill, P(draw X), …).

- `Facts` will be the only source of narration ([Output §5](/en/arch/output/#sec-5)). NoSL facts are probabilities and expectations; SL facts may be exact.
- `ICombatPolicy` / `IChoicePolicy` stay, for the placeholders and for rollout policies (fast policies that play a line out inside search).

## 7. Tests

1. Tactics suite: positions cut from simulated games that have clear answers (take lethal, exact block, apply vulnerable before attacking); record found / not found.
2. Fixed combat panel (encounter, deck, seed) with CRN (common random numbers: every version plays the same random draws, so differences come from the brain, not from luck): mean HP loss and death rate. First beat the placeholders; version gates as in [Evaluation §5](/en/arch/evaluation/#sec-5). Built: `panel` ([§7.1](#sec-7.1)).
3. NoSL counterfactual: same record, different hidden state → same recommendation (fixed brain seed).
4. SL seal: any execute past the node exit throws. Holds (`selftest`): a node's clone loads at its horizon and refuses another step, and the SL world refuses any action after its combat's end.
5. Reproducible with a fixed branch budget. Holds: two panel runs give the same moves and results.
6. SL plan fidelity: the real combat follows the plan; a divergence means the clone is wrong. Holds (`selftest`): the plan of a run's combat, made on the node's clone, is played in the run step for step, each observation as the plan expects, and the combat ends with the HP and potions the plan's value says; the pooled planner plans the same as in one process. In runs a step that does not hold is counted as a replan ([§3](#sec-3)).
7. SL planner, without the game (`Sts2Ai.Tests`): on a small exact combat, a full search finds the best line that trying every line finds, and the line replays to the value it claims; a losing plan stops at its first turn's end unless it is proved, and a proved loss replays to its end.

## 8. Open questions

1. K and nodes per world: SPSA tunes them ([§5](#sec-5)); the panel shows the marginal HP-loss gain per added world.
2. How bad fusion is, which a fusion panel decides: 300–500 NoSL decisions from the evaluation logs, rich in reveals within the turn (draws, random targets, generated cards), K = 32–64 worlds, the turn enumerated in full. For each first action it compares the PIMC value (the mean over worlds of each world's best) with the value when backups are merged by observation history (max over actions, mean over the worlds consistent with that history). If they disagree on fewer than about 3% of the decisions and the loss is within the noise, there is no ISMCTS. Why: αμ changed only 1.3–3% of decisions, EPIMC gained nothing in games whose information is mostly public, and one tree simulation costs about 3–4 DFS nodes (estimated).
   The panel is built: `fusion --log FILE[,…] [--k 32] [--max-nodes 5000]` takes the logs' NoSL combat decisions, with the observation and seed each one logged, and builds its worlds as pimc does. Worlds count as showing the same observation when they differ only in uids: a drawn card's uid is its place in that world's shuffle, so it differs between worlds that draw the same card. For the same reason actions are compared by the card's place in the hand. PIMC's pick loses the merged value of the merged pick less its own; the panel reports how often that is above 0, overall and over the decisions with a reveal inside the turn. A decision with a world over `--max-nodes` states is skipped and counted. A first try on 36 decisions from two runs (K = 16): 8 with a reveal, one pick worse, mean loss 0.00002; a complex turn takes about a minute.
3. NoSL leaf: the turn-end `LeafValue`, a rollout to the end of the combat, and the outcome head ([§4](#sec-4)), at equal wall-clock time. A rollout at every leaf is too expensive, since almost every DFS node carries an end-turn leaf: only the two first actions SH keeps last get one rollout per world, from their best line, as a confirmation.
4. SL: beam width B and the nodes per state. Measured so far ([§5](#sec-5), [§7.1](#sec-7.1)): beam 8 × 300 proves 18% of its plans on act 1 weak + regular, 3% on elites and 1% on act 2, and on won combats costs about what PIMC NoSL does. Open: what a wider beam or more nodes buy in whole runs, and the time a loss takes, since an unproved loss is planned again every turn; equal wall-clock time with NoSL (open question 2 in [Environments §10](/en/arch/environments/#sec-10)).
5. Leaf v0 against the feature leaf tuned by SPSA, on the panel.
