[Turn search](/en/arch/sl-planner/turn-search/) searches one turn from one state in full. A fight is many turns, and searching every turn of every line in full would multiply without end. The planner keeps only the most promising states from each turn, by the leaf's value. [§1](#sec-1)–[§6](#sec-6) are the code as it runs: the beam over turns (`SlPlanner.Search`), why nothing else cuts it, and the rules for picking, proving and replanning a line. [§7](#sec-7) is the planned search that takes the dive's and the beam's place.

## 1. A beam over turns

**Beam search** is breadth-first search that keeps only the best $B$ states at each depth and expands only those. Here a depth is a turn. With $S_0 = \{s_0\}$, the decision's state:

$$
S_{t+1} = \operatorname{top}_B \Big\{\, e \in \bigcup_{s \in S_t} \operatorname{ends}(s) \ :\ e \text{ still in progress} \Big\}
$$

where $\operatorname{ends}(s)$ is what the turn's search from $s$ returns, $\operatorname{top}_B$ takes the $B$ best by the leaf's value, distinct by key, and every end that finishes the fight is a line for the pick instead ([§5](#sec-5)). The sort is stable, so ties keep the order in which their states were searched, and the beam does not depend on which worker answered first. The beam goes on until no state is left, or for at most 40 turns.

$B$ is `Budget.Beam` (`--beam`): 8 at every effort level but max, which has 16 ([Budget §1](/en/arch/sl-planner/budget/#sec-1)). Each kept state's turn is searched with the budget's nodes per state and turn; $B$ and the nodes are the same in every room.

A turn usually ends in many more states than the beam keeps: of a turn's distinct ends, only the best 8 survive across all 8 kept states. That is the planner's main approximation, and why most of its plans are not proved ([§5](#sec-5)).

## 2. No bound

The beam's width is the only cut. A state is never dropped because its lines could not beat the best line found so far, since no value of a state caps what its lines can reach. The leaf prices a win by V of the run the fight leaves ([Combat brain: the leaf §2](/en/arch/combat-brain/leaf/#sec-2)), and a line can still raise that past winning now with what the state holds: max HP or gold gained in the fight, a card grown, a potion drunk that V rates below an empty slot. A bound that assumed the fight can only lose HP and use up potions would prune lines better than the best (`SlPlanner`, `src/Sts2Ai.Brain/Combat/SlPlanner.cs`).

So every state the beam keeps is searched on, every line that ends the fight is weighed for the pick, and a plan is proved only by a full beam that left nothing out ([§5](#sec-5)).

## 3. The greedy dive

Before the full beam, the planner runs a beam of one: at each turn it keeps the single best state by the leaf. This **greedy dive** reaches the end of the fight in one line. It does two things for the full beam:

- When the dive wins, the fight is already won, and the full beam narrows to the easy beam ([§4](#sec-4)). When the dive proves its line, which takes turns searched in full that each leave a single state going on, the full beam is skipped.
- The dive asks each of its turns for every end and keeps them. The full beam passes through the same states early on (its first turn always), and takes those turns' ends from the dive instead of searching them again; the plan is the one a fresh search would give ([Turn search §4](/en/arch/sl-planner/turn-search/#sec-4)).

## 4. The easy beam

When the dive wins, the full beam can only find a win that leaves the run better. So it keeps $E$ states a turn instead of $B$ (`Budget.EasyBeam`, `--easy-beam`; 4 at medium effort, 2 at low). When the dive loses, the full beam keeps $B$: then it is looking for a win at all.

The easy beam is most of medium effort's saving over high, which keeps $B$ always ([Budget §1](/en/arch/sl-planner/budget/#sec-1)). It narrows only the fights the dive already wins: whenever the dive loses, the full beam keeps the full width.

## 5. Picking the line, and proving it

The pick is the best line that ends the fight, unless the beam stopped at its turn cap at a state the leaf values higher. Every loss is worth 0, so among losing lines the pick is the one that died from the turn start the leaf valued highest: the line that lasted in the best shape, where the fight is most likely to turn if the plan was wrong.

A plan is **proved** when nothing better can exist: the last beam left nothing out. No turn's search was cut (out of nodes, or more ends than it kept), no state was dropped from the beam, and every line ended the fight (`Beam.Proved`).

**What the host is shown.** Besides the pick, the planner keeps, for each first action its kept lines began with, the best of those lines. Those are its candidates, the pick first: each with the line's value, its chance to win the fight and the HP held at a win, where the line ends the fight 1 or 0 and the exact HP, elsewhere the leaf's expectation ([Turn search §4](/en/arch/sl-planner/turn-search/#sec-4)). The mod shows them as the fight's chance to win and HP left ([Output §2](/en/arch/output/#sec-2)).

Proofs are rare: a turn yields many more states than the beam keeps.

## 6. Losing plans

A plan that loses and is not proved may be wrong: the beam may have dropped the state that leads to a win. The planner treats such a plan as a guess:

- **Replan.** An unproved losing plan is played for its first `--replan` turns (1 by default) and then the next turn is planned again. From there the rest of the fight is shorter, so the same beam reaches deeper into it.
- **At most L in a row.** The $L$-th unproved losing plan in a row of one fight is played to its end (`--loss-plans`, 3 at low and medium). Each plan is a whole search again, and a fight that keeps planning to lose is most likely lost: without a limit, a lost fight would be planned again at every turn, and lost fights would take most of a run's time.
- **Proved losses** are played to their end: no line does better.

The route keeps the count (`SlRoute`): a fight ends its run of losing plans with its first plan that is not an unproved loss.

So at low or medium effort a fight whose plans keep losing is planned three times: two plans played one turn each, then the third played to its end.

## 7. Iterative widening <Badge text="planned" variant="caution" />

The design runs one loop in place of the dive, the beam and the rules around them. Nothing here is implemented or measured.

```text
best = none
for width in 1, 2, 4, 8, ...:              # each pass runs to the fight's end
    states = [decision]
    while states:
        ends = TurnEnds of each state       # shared table: a state is searched once
        best = the better of best and the lines that ended the fight
        states = the top `width` ends by the leaf
    stop if proved, or best wins and width >= 4, or the fight's nodes are spent
play best
```

Width 1 is the dive of [§3](#sec-3); a later pass is a beam of that width, which takes the turns the passes before it searched from them. The figure runs the loop on the made-up fight of [§4](#sec-4). A turn searched in an earlier pass costs no nodes again, so a wider pass pays only for what is new.

### 7.1 The rules

- **Top width by the leaf.** The leaf ranks the ends, and nothing is pruned by value ([§2](#sec-2), [Combat brain: the leaf](/en/arch/combat-brain/leaf/)).
- **Stop** when the line is proved, or won with a width of at least 4, or the fight's nodes are spent. A pass cut by the budget still improves `best` with any line it finished.
- **A losing plan** is played for one turn and planned again, until the fight's nodes are spent; then the last plan is played to its end. There is no count of plans.
- **Proof** means what it means in [§5](#sec-5): a pass left nothing out.

### 7.2 Why widening, and not depth

A pass of any width ends in a complete, real line, so the search can be stopped at any budget with something to play. The alternative, a depth-first branch and bound, may hold no complete line better than its first for a long time, and nodes here are dear: each is an action of the game's own code, and each step back a restore ([Budget §3](/en/arch/sl-planner/budget/#sec-3)). Alpha-beta has nothing to offer either, since its gains come from an opponent's replies and a fight has none: the enemies' moves are fixed inside the one world. A comparison with depth-first search is open.

### 7.3 What it replaces

| built | in the design |
|---|---|
| greedy dive ([§3](#sec-3)) | the pass of width 1 |
| beam $B$ ([§1](#sec-1)) | widths grow until a stop; hard fights spend more because they do not stop early |
| easy beam $E$ ([§4](#sec-4)) | stop at a win with width at least 4 |
| `--replan`, `--loss-plans` ([§6](#sec-6)) | one turn of a losing plan, until the fight's nodes are spent |
| one turn's transpositions ([Turn search §2](/en/arch/sl-planner/turn-search/#sec-2)), the dive's turns kept for the beam | one table across turns and passes |
