The planner's budget is a few numbers: the beam $B$, the easy beam $E$, the nodes per state and turn, how many turns of a losing plan are played before planning again, and how many losing plans are allowed in a row ([Beam](/en/arch/sl-planner/beam/)). [§1](#sec-1)–[§6](#sec-6) are how those numbers are set, what they cost, and what the champion plays with. [§7](#sec-7) is the planned budget, one number per fight.

## 1. Effort levels

`--effort low|medium|high|max` sets the planner's budget in one option (`Budget.Effort`); an option given beside it (`--beam`, `--easy-beam`, `--nodes`, `--replan`, `--loss-plans`) overrides its part. Medium is the default, and the SL champion plays it. Every level searches 300 nodes per state and turn and plays an unproved losing plan for one turn before planning again:

| effort | beam $B$ | easy beam $E$ | losing plans $L$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| low | 8 | 2 | 3 |
| medium | 8 | 4 | 3 |
| high | 8 | 8 | no limit |
| max | 16 | 16 | no limit |

Medium differs from high in two places: the easy beam, which narrows only fights the dive already wins ([Beam §4](/en/arch/sl-planner/beam/#sec-4)), and the limit on losing plans, which keeps a lost fight from being planned again at every turn ([Beam §6](/en/arch/sl-planner/beam/#sec-6)). Low narrows the easy beam further. Every level gives every room the same beam and nodes.

**A room's own level.** `--elite-effort LEVEL` and `--boss-effort LEVEL` give elite fights and boss fights a level of their own, by the table above; every other fight keeps the budget the other options make (`src/Sts2Ai.SimWorker/Commands/Args.cs:126-129`, `:155-157`). A room's level that equals that budget changes nothing and is not kept. The budget holds them as `Budget.Elite` and `Budget.Boss`, and `Budget.For` gives a fight the budget of its room, which the fight's observation names: `Monster`, `Elite` or `Boss`, an event's fight by its encounter (`src/Sts2Ai.Brain/Combat/CombatBrain.cs:17-27`). The route plans every fight with its room's budget, its loss plans counted by that budget too (`SlRoute`, `src/Sts2Ai.Brain/Combat/SlPlanner.cs:43`), and the decision log records the budget the fight had ([Harness: the decision log §3](/en/arch/harness/decision-log/#sec-3)). The champion's name shows a room's budget after the others', as in `beam 8x300 easy 4 loss plans 3 boss (beam 8x300)`, and a champion file keeps the levels as `elite-effort` and `boss-effort` ([Evaluation: champions and baselines §1](/en/arch/evaluation/champions/#sec-1)). The champion plays medium in every room; a dataset may play its boss fights at high ([Macro brain: self-play §2](/en/arch/macro-brain/selfplay/#sec-2)).

## 2. The low budget

A run may also play below every effort level, with the options set by hand. The **low budget** is:

```bash
Sts2Ai.SimWorker run --env SL --policy plan --effort low --nodes 150 --beam 4 --easy-beam 0 --loss-plans 0 --replan 0 …
```

That is a beam of 4 at 150 nodes per state and turn, the full width always (an easy beam of 0 means $B$), and every plan played to its end (`--replan 0`), so a fight is planned once. It is not the low effort level of [§1](#sec-1), `--effort low` alone, which searches 300 nodes per state and turn with a beam of 8 and plans a losing fight again. The champion, its self-play and its gates play medium ([Macro brain: self-play §2](/en/arch/macro-brain/selfplay/#sec-2), [Evaluation: gates §10](/en/arch/evaluation/gates/#sec-10)).

## 3. Where the time goes

**Restores.** A turn's search starts with a restore: the worker loads the node's clone and replays the state's path from the decision. Each step back inside the search is a restore too, since the depth-first search goes back to a state before each of its other actions (`TurnDfs.Visit`, `src/Sts2Ai.Brain/Combat/SlPlanner.cs`). An SL restore loads the node's checkpoint and replays every step since the node began, so it costs more late in a long fight ([Simulator: worlds and the SL clone §7](/en/arch/simulator/worlds/#sec-7)). Restoring states, not the leaf, is the largest share of a worker's time ([Combat brain: prompts, moves and workers §3](/en/arch/combat-brain/shared/#sec-3)). So what a plan costs grows with the states the beam keeps, the plans a fight makes and the nodes each turn's search may spend: the budget's three levers.

## 4. Workers

The turns' searches of one beam step are independent, so each kept state's turn is one request to the worker pool (`SlTurnRequest`, [Combat brain: prompts, moves and workers §3](/en/arch/combat-brain/shared/#sec-3)): the worker loads the node's clone, plays the state's path from the decision, searches the turn and returns its best ends. `PooledPlanner` sends one request per kept state for each turn of the beam and merges the replies in request order. A turn's search lives in one request, so a plan is the same on any number of workers as in one process, which `selftest --workers N` checks. A run's pool has `--workers` processes, 8 by default (one per world of `--worlds`).

## 5. The champion

The SL champion is the planner at medium effort (beam 8 × 300, easy beam 4, 3 losing plans) with the champion's combat net in its leaves, and L0 with the champion's V on the clone (`eval/champions/sl.json`, [Evaluation: champions and baselines §1](/en/arch/evaluation/champions/#sec-1)). Its reports are one per character, each 100 SL runs at ascension 0 on the seeds `promotion-1-100`, at that budget; their wins and floors are in [Evaluation: champions and baselines §2](/en/arch/evaluation/champions/#sec-2). No run failed or was cut short, and no decision fell to a placeholder.

What the budget costs differs by character:

| character | ms per decision | s per run |
|---|---|---|
| Silent | 405 | 336 |
| Ironclad | 431 | 286 |
| Defect | 560 | 423 |
| Regent | 1,202 | 906 |
| Necrobinder | 1,358 | 1,046 |

## 6. Weaknesses and open questions

- **A narrow beam.** A turn ends in far more distinct states than the 8 the beam keeps, so few plans are proved and a better line can fall outside the beam. What a wider beam or more nodes buy in whole runs (`--effort max`) is not measured. Iterative widening is the planned answer ([Beam §7](/en/arch/sl-planner/beam/#sec-7)).
- **What the leaf cannot see.** The potions a fight ends with follow its HP only by groups of 10 buckets, each potion taken apart from the others, and what is still to come in the fight besides its HP and potions (gold stolen, max HP lost) is priced only once it has happened ([Combat brain: the leaf §7](/en/arch/combat-brain/leaf/#sec-7)).
- **Prompts on a share.** The answers of an action's first card prompt share that action's nodes, so a prompt with many answers is searched thinly, and a second prompt of the same action takes its first options ([Turn search §3](/en/arch/sl-planner/turn-search/#sec-3), [Combat brain: prompts, moves and workers §1](/en/arch/combat-brain/shared/#sec-1)).
- **The fight's end is the horizon.** The search stops where the fight does, valued by the leaf; the rewards after it and the rest of the node, which SL may also see, are left to the macro brain. Searching on to the node's exit is open ([Environments: information §9](/en/arch/environments/information/#sec-9), question 1).
- **Lost fights cost time.** A fight whose plan loses is planned again, up to three plans at medium, each a whole search ([Beam §6](/en/arch/sl-planner/beam/#sec-6)). The limit on losing plans caps it; no cheaper test tells a lost fight from a hard one.
- **Fair comparison with NoSL.** The planner gets the same nodes per search, not the same time. Equal wall-clock time is the fairer budget for comparing the environments ([Environments: information §9](/en/arch/environments/information/#sec-9), question 2).

## 7. One budget per fight <Badge text="planned" variant="caution" />

In the design a fight has one node budget, and the search has one minimum width, 4 in the stop rule of [Beam §7](/en/arch/sl-planner/beam/#sec-7). These two replace the dive, the easy beam, the loss plans, the replan options and the effort presets' other parts. The sizes are to be set by gates; none is chosen or measured.

- **It spends where the fight is hard.** An easy fight stops at width 4 with a win or earlier on a proof. A hard one, with no win yet, goes on widening and planning again until its nodes are spent, which is what the loss plans approximate.
- **It is counted in nodes, so it stays comparable across machines.** As a scale, a turn of the full beam at medium searches about 8 × 300 nodes ([§1](#sec-1)).
- **Self-play and gates can differ**, each with its own number, as a run can take the low budget instead of an effort level ([§2](#sec-2)).
- **Workers stay.** Each state's `TurnEnds` is one request. A worker keeps nothing between requests ([Combat brain: prompts, moves and workers §3](/en/arch/combat-brain/shared/#sec-3)), so the table shared across turns and passes has to live in the host or in a request's reply; that choice is left to the implementation.
