This page takes one real state and walks it through the combat net by hand, with the numbers the net computes for it. Read [The combat net](/en/arch/combat-brain/network/) for the design; here each step shows what that design does to one fight. Vectors show their first four numbers.

The state is a round start from the file's own held-out test states (`weights/all/c9-1.json`, `tests[9]`). Loading the net checks that it gives this state exactly these outputs ([The combat net §9](/en/arch/combat-brain/network/#sec-9)). The numbers come from `docs/scripts/combat-walk.py`, which stops if its layers do not end in the file's outputs.

## 4. One card, then the piles

The card's vector is the first lines of `Net.encode` (`learn/combat/net.py:229-308`). Take **{walk.card.id}** in the hand. Its entry in the card table is {walk.card.entry}, its upgrade level {walk.card.up}; it costs 1, which reads as {walk.card.cost}, and its damage of 6 reads as {walk.card.damage}. Its other numbers (block, calculated number, replays, star cost, gathered damage) are 0, and it has no modifiers, so only these parts are left:

| part | vector |
|---|---|
| its id's vector $E[c]$ | <code>{v(walk.card.id_vector)}</code> |
| its upgrade direction $U[c]$, times {walk.card.up} | <code>{v(walk.card.up_vector)}</code> |
| the cost vector $K$, times {walk.card.cost} | <code>{v(walk.card.cost_vector)}</code> |
| the damage vector $D$, times {walk.card.damage} | <code>{v(walk.card.damage_vector)}</code> |
| its own damage direction $D_c[c]$, times {walk.card.damage} | <code>{v(walk.card.card_damage_vector)}</code> |
| the hand's vector $P[l]$ | <code>{v(walk.card.pile_vector)}</code> |
| their sum $x$ | <code>{v(walk.card.x)}</code> |

Add the first numbers up to check: 0.228 + 0 + 0.151 − 0.023 + 0.001 − 0.141 = 0.216. The damage's two parts add −0.022 to the first number, against the id's 0.228 ([The combat net §3](/en/arch/combat-brain/network/#sec-3)).

$\varphi$ then maps $x$ to 128 numbers, <code>{v(walk.card.phi.first)}</code>, of length {walk.card.phi.norm}. Being a ReLU's output, none is below 0, and {walk.card.phi.zeros} of the 128 are exactly 0.

Every card goes through the same $\varphi$, and each pile adds its cards up and scales by 1/8:

| pile | cards | first numbers | length | numbers at 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hand | {walk.piles[0].cards} | <code>{v(walk.piles[0].vector.first)}</code> | {walk.piles[0].vector.norm} | {walk.piles[0].vector.zeros} |
| draw | {walk.piles[1].cards} | <code>{v(walk.piles[1].vector.first)}</code> | {walk.piles[1].vector.norm} | {walk.piles[1].vector.zeros} |
| discard | {walk.piles[2].cards} | <code>{v(walk.piles[2].vector.first)}</code> | {walk.piles[2].vector.norm} | {walk.piles[2].vector.zeros} |
| exhaust | {walk.piles[3].cards} | <code>{v(walk.piles[3].vector.first)}</code> | {walk.piles[3].vector.norm} | {walk.piles[3].vector.zeros} |

The exhaust pile is empty, and an empty pile is exactly the zero vector. These four join into the 512 numbers of the cards.

## 10. The endings head: 51 logits

One linear layer gives 51 raw numbers. The first is the death's, {walk.head.death_logit}. A logit has no meaning alone: only the softmax in the next step, which compares them, makes them chances.

## 11. The mask, then the softmax

The HP share is {walk.value.share}. A bucket $[l, l + 0.025)$ stays open if $l < 0.839$ and $l + 0.025 > 0.839 - 1$. Here:

- The bottom: a fight cannot heal more than the HP missing. {walk.state.max_hp - walk.state.hp} of {walk.state.max_hp} HP is a share of 0.161, so the 3 buckets below {walk.head.open_from} are closed.
- The top: a win cannot lose all the HP held, so the buckets from {walk.head.open_to} up are closed.

That leaves **{walk.head.open} of the 50 buckets open**. The others get a logit of $-10^4$ (`Net.reachable`, `learn/combat/net.py:221-227`), and the softmax gives them exactly 0.

The softmax over the rest gives the chances. A death has {walk.head.death}. The most likely buckets, where a share below 0 is HP gained:

| share of max HP still to be lost | in HP | chance |
|---|---|---|
| {walk.head.tops[0].low} to {walk.head.tops[0].high} | 4.7 to 7.0 gained | {pct(walk.head.tops[0].chance)} |
| {walk.head.tops[1].low} to {walk.head.tops[1].high} | 2.3 to 4.7 gained | {pct(walk.head.tops[1].chance)} |
| {walk.head.tops[2].low} to {walk.head.tops[2].high} | 7.0 to 9.3 gained | {pct(walk.head.tops[2].chance)} |
| {walk.head.tops[3].low} to {walk.head.tops[3].high} | 0 to 2.3 gained | {pct(walk.head.tops[3].chance)} |

The net expects this fight to be won and to end with more HP than now: almost two thirds of the chance falls on a gain of 4.7 to 7.0 HP. Burning Blood heals 6 HP after every won fight, a share of 0.065, inside that bucket. The net read the relics among its inputs, though nothing here says which inputs moved it most.

## 12. The potion head

The same 512 numbers go through a second linear layer, of 512 → {walk.potion.outputs}, one output per entry of the potion table. Fairy in a Bottle is entry {walk.potion.entry}. Its logit is {walk.potion.logit}, and the sigmoid makes that **{walk.potion.chance}** to four places, about 2.5 in a million: the net expects the potion to be still held after a win. The player never drinks this potion; the game uses it on its own when the player would die, and brings the player back with 30 % of max HP.

The other {walk.potion.outputs - 1} outputs are computed and not read. The largest here are {walk.potion.most_likely[0].id} and {walk.potion.most_likely[1].id}, at {walk.potion.most_likely[0].chance} and {walk.potion.most_likely[1].chance}: this state holds neither, so they mean nothing and train nothing. A potion that no training held, such as {walk.potion.unheld}, has `<unk>`'s row and shows its chance, {walk.potion.unk}.

## 13. The number the search reads

`CombatNet.Value` (`src/Sts2Ai.Brain/Combat/CombatNet.cs:74-85`) prices the 51 chances ([The leaf §5](/en/arch/combat-brain/leaf/#sec-5)):

$$
\text{value} = \sum_{k=1}^{50} p_k \big(0.2 + 0.6\,(s - m_k)\big) + (1 - p_0)\times 0.02 \sum_{\text{held}} (1 - u_{\text{id}})
$$

| term | number |
|---|---|
| the endings, summed over the open buckets | {walk.value.win_term} |
| potions expected to be kept, $\sum (1 - u)$ | {walk.value.kept} |
| their term, $(1 - p_0) \times 0.02 \times$ that | {walk.value.potion_term} |
| **the value** | **{walk.value.value}** |

The planner's **bound** for this state is a win with no HP or potion lost: 0.2 + 0.6 × {walk.value.share} + 0.02 = {walk.value.bound}. The value sits 0.034 above it, since the net expects the fight to end about 5 HP above where it stands: a share of 0.056, which at 0.6 is 0.034. This is the case [The leaf §5](/en/arch/combat-brain/leaf/#sec-5) allows for: a net that predicts healing can rise above the bound.

| as it is | {walk.value.share} | {walk.value.win_term} | {walk.value.potion_term} | {walk.value.value} |

## 16. The label

So far the net has predicted. In training this state also has an answer, from how its fight really ended. The file does not keep that ending, so take one for illustration: **suppose the fight is won at {walk.label.end_hp} of 93 HP, with the potion still held**.

- **The ending's class.** The share lost from this state is 78/93 − {walk.label.end_hp}/93 = {walk.label.lost}, in the bucket from {walk.label.low} to {walk.label.high}: class {walk.label.class}. Every round start of that fight would get its own class, from its own HP.
- **The potion's label.** It is still in the first run view after the fight, so its label is 0.
- **The loss.** The chance of class {walk.label.class} was {walk.label.chance}, so the endings' cross-entropy is {walk.label.ending_loss}. The potion's is {walk.label.potion_loss}. The sample's loss is their sum, and a gradient step would push that bucket's chance up.

A large loss like the ending's is what a surprise looks like: under this supposed ending, the net was badly wrong about the HP, and not about the potion. How labels are made across a whole list of runs is in [Training the combat net §3](/en/arch/learning/combat-net/#sec-3).
