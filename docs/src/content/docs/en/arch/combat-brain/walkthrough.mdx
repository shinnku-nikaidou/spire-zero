This page takes one real state and walks it through the combat net by hand, with the numbers the net computes for it. Read [The combat net](/en/arch/combat-brain/network/) for the design; here each step shows what that design does to one fight. Vectors show their first four numbers.

The state is a round start from the file's own held-out test states (`weights/silent/c8-2.json`, `tests[7]`). Loading the net checks that it gives this state exactly these outputs ([The combat net §9](/en/arch/combat-brain/network/#sec-9)). The numbers come from `docs/scripts/combat-walk.py`, which stops if its layers do not end in the file's outputs.

## 4. One card, then the piles

The card's vector is the first lines of `Net.encode` (`learn/combat/net.py:211-282`). Take **{walk.card.id}** in the hand. Its entry in the card table is {walk.card.entry}, its upgrade level {walk.card.up}; it costs 0, and its damage of 6 reads as {walk.card.damage}.

| part | vector |
|---|---|
| its id's vector $E[c]$ | <code>{v(walk.card.id_vector)}</code> |
| its upgrade direction $U[c]$, times {walk.card.up} | <code>{v(walk.card.up_vector)}</code> |
| the cost vector $K$, times {walk.card.cost} | <code>{v(walk.card.cost_vector)}</code> |
| the damage vector $D$, times {walk.card.damage} | <code>{v(walk.card.damage_vector)}</code> |
| the hand's vector $P[l]$ | <code>{v(walk.card.pile_vector)}</code> |
| their sum $x$ | <code>{v(walk.card.x)}</code> |

Add the first numbers up to check: 0.065 + 0 + 0 − 0.004 − 0.138 = −0.078, off by the rounding. $D$ is small in this file, so the damage adds little ([The combat net §8](/en/arch/combat-brain/network/#sec-8)).

$\varphi$ then maps $x$ to 64 numbers, <code>{v(walk.card.phi.first)}</code>. Being a ReLU's output, none is below 0, and {walk.card.phi.zeros} of the 64 are exactly 0.

Every card goes through the same $\varphi$, and each pile adds its cards up and scales by 1/8:

| pile | cards | first numbers | length | numbers at 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hand | {walk.piles[0].cards} | <code>{v(walk.piles[0].vector.first)}</code> | {walk.piles[0].vector.norm} | {walk.piles[0].vector.zeros} |
| draw | {walk.piles[1].cards} | <code>{v(walk.piles[1].vector.first)}</code> | {walk.piles[1].vector.norm} | {walk.piles[1].vector.zeros} |
| discard | {walk.piles[2].cards} | <code>{v(walk.piles[2].vector.first)}</code> | {walk.piles[2].vector.norm} | {walk.piles[2].vector.zeros} |
| exhaust | {walk.piles[3].cards} | <code>{v(walk.piles[3].vector.first)}</code> | {walk.piles[3].vector.norm} | {walk.piles[3].vector.zeros} |

An empty pile is exactly the zero vector. These four join into the 256 numbers of the cards.

## 5. The powers

## 10. The endings head: 51 logits

One linear layer gives 51 raw numbers. The first is the death's, {walk.head.death_logit}. A logit has no meaning alone: only the softmax in the next step, which compares them, makes them chances.

## 11. The mask, then the softmax

The HP share is {walk.value.share}. A bucket $[l, l + 0.025)$ stays open if $l < 0.5571$ and $l + 0.025 > 0.5571 - 1$. Here:

- The bottom: a fight cannot heal more than the HP missing. {walk.state.max_hp - walk.state.hp} of {walk.state.max_hp} HP is a share of 0.443, more than the lowest bucket's {walk.head.open_from}, so no bucket closes here.
- The top: a win cannot lose all the HP held, so the buckets from {walk.head.open_to} up are closed.

That leaves **{walk.head.open} of the 50 buckets open**. The others get a logit of $-10^4$ (`Net.reachable`, `learn/combat/net.py:203-209`), and the softmax gives them exactly 0.

The softmax over the rest gives the chances. A death has {walk.head.death}. The most likely buckets:

| share of max HP still to be lost | in HP | chance |
|---|---|---|
| {walk.head.tops[0].low} to {walk.head.tops[0].high} | 21 to 22.8 | {pct(walk.head.tops[0].chance)} |
| {walk.head.tops[1].low} to {walk.head.tops[1].high} | 22.8 to 24.5 | {pct(walk.head.tops[1].chance)} |
| {walk.head.tops[2].low} to {walk.head.tops[2].high} | 17.5 to 19.3 | {pct(walk.head.tops[2].chance)} |
| {walk.head.tops[3].low} to {walk.head.tops[3].high} | 24.5 to 26.3 | {pct(walk.head.tops[3].chance)} |

The net expects this fight to be won at a real cost, about a third of max HP. The enemy holds 334 HP with 25 poison, and the player's Noxious Fumes adds more each turn; the net read those numbers, though nothing here says which inputs moved it most.

## 12. The potion head

The same 256 numbers go through a second linear layer, of 256 → {walk.potion.outputs}, one output per entry of the potion table. Fairy in a Bottle is entry {walk.potion.entry}. Its logit is {walk.potion.logit}, and the sigmoid makes that **{walk.potion.chance}**: the net expects the potion to be gone after a win.

The other {walk.potion.outputs - 1} outputs are computed and not read. The largest here are {walk.potion.most_likely[0].id} and {walk.potion.most_likely[1].id}, both near {walk.potion.most_likely[0].chance}: this state holds neither, so they mean nothing and train nothing. A potion that neither training held, such as {walk.potion.unheld}, has `<unk>`'s row and shows its chance, {walk.potion.unk}.

## 13. The number the search reads

`CombatNet.Value` (`src/Sts2Ai.Brain/Combat/CombatNet.cs:74-86`) prices the 51 chances ([The leaf §5](/en/arch/combat-brain/leaf/#sec-5)):

$$
\text{value} = \sum_{k=1}^{50} p_k \big(0.2 + 0.6\,(s - m_k)\big) + (1 - p_0)\times 0.02 \sum_{\text{held}} (1 - u_{\text{id}})
$$

| term | number |
|---|---|
| the endings, summed over the open buckets | {walk.value.win_term} |
| potions expected to be kept, $\sum (1 - u)$ | {walk.value.kept} |
| their term, $(1 - p_0) \times 0.02 \times$ that | {walk.value.potion_term} |
| **the value** | **{walk.value.value}** |

The planner's **bound** for this state is a win with no HP or potion lost: 0.2 + 0.6 × {walk.value.share} + 0.02 = {walk.value.bound}. The value sits far below it, since the net expects a third of max HP and the potion to be lost.

## 14. What a search sees

A search compares states. Change one thing at a time and run the net again:

| state | HP share | endings | potions | value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| as it is | {walk.value.share} | {walk.value.win_term} | {walk.value.potion_term} | {walk.value.value} |
| {walk.lost_hp} HP less | {walk.siblings.less_hp.share} | {walk.siblings.less_hp.win_term} | {walk.siblings.less_hp.potion_term} | {walk.siblings.less_hp.value} |
| the potion gone | {walk.siblings.no_potion.share} | {walk.siblings.no_potion.win_term} | {walk.siblings.no_potion.potion_term} | {walk.siblings.no_potion.value} |

{walk.lost_hp} HP is a share of 0.143 of 70, which at 0.6 is 0.086, while the value falls by 0.041. The HP held counts in full ([The combat net §7](/en/arch/combat-brain/network/#sec-7)), and the net answers as well: it now expects less to be lost, the likeliest bucket moving down to {walk.siblings.less_hp.tops[0].low} to {walk.siblings.less_hp.tops[0].high}, with a death at {pct(walk.siblings.less_hp.death)}.

The potion's flat price says nothing here, since the net expects it to be used. Yet without it a death goes from {pct(walk.value.death)} to {pct(walk.siblings.no_potion.death)}, and the value falls to {walk.siblings.no_potion.value}. The net has learned what Fairy in a Bottle does in this fight, from the endings of runs that held it.

## 16. The label

So far the net has predicted. In training this state also has an answer, from how its fight really ended. The file does not keep that ending, so take one for illustration: **suppose the fight is won at {walk.label.end_hp} of 70 HP, with the potion still held**.

- **The ending's class.** The share lost from this state is 39/70 − {walk.label.end_hp}/70 = {walk.label.lost}, in the bucket from {walk.label.low} to {walk.label.high}: class {walk.label.class}. Every round start of that fight would get its own class, from its own HP.
- **The potion's label.** It is still in the first run view after the fight, so its label is 0.
- **The loss.** The chance of class {walk.label.class} was {walk.label.chance}, so the endings' cross-entropy is {walk.label.ending_loss}. The potion's is {walk.label.potion_loss}. The sample's loss is their sum, and a gradient step would push that bucket's chance up.

A large loss like the potion's is what a surprise looks like: under this supposed ending, the net was badly wrong about the potion, and not about the HP. How labels are made across a whole list of runs is in [Training the combat net §3](/en/arch/learning/combat-net/#sec-3).
