export const v = (x) => '[' + x.join(', ') + ', …]';
export const pct = (x) => (100 * x).toFixed(2) + ' %';

This page takes one real state and walks it through the combat net by hand, with the numbers the net computes for it. Read [The combat net](/en/arch/combat-brain/network/) for the design; here each step shows what that design does to one fight. Vectors show their first four numbers.

The state is a round start from the file's own held-out test states (`weights/silent/c7-2.json`, `tests[1]`). Loading the net checks that it gives this state exactly these outputs ([The combat net §9](/en/arch/combat-brain/network/#sec-9)). The numbers come from `docs/scripts/combat-walk.py`, which stops if its layers do not end in the file's outputs.

## 4. One card, then the piles

Take **{walk.card.id}+** in the hand. Its entry in the card table is {walk.card.entry}, its upgrade level {walk.card.up}, and it costs 0.

| part | vector |
|---|---|
| its id's vector $E[c]$ | <code>{v(walk.card.id_vector)}</code> |
| its upgrade direction $U[c]$, times {walk.card.up} | <code>{v(walk.card.up_vector)}</code> |
| the cost vector $K$, times {walk.card.cost} | <code>{v(walk.card.cost_vector)}</code> |
| the hand's vector $P[l]$ | <code>{v(walk.card.pile_vector)}</code> |
| their sum $x$ | <code>{v(walk.card.x)}</code> |

Add the first numbers up to check: −0.134 + 0.108 + 0 − 0.132 = −0.158.

$\varphi$ then maps $x$ to 64 numbers, <code>{v(walk.card.phi.first)}</code>. Being a ReLU's output, none is below 0, and {walk.card.phi.zeros} of the 64 are exactly 0.

Every card goes through the same $\varphi$, and each pile adds its cards up and scales by 1/8:

| pile | cards | first numbers | length | numbers at 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hand | {walk.piles[0].cards} | <code>{v(walk.piles[0].vector.first)}</code> | {walk.piles[0].vector.norm} | {walk.piles[0].vector.zeros} |
| draw | {walk.piles[1].cards} | <code>{v(walk.piles[1].vector.first)}</code> | {walk.piles[1].vector.norm} | {walk.piles[1].vector.zeros} |
| discard | {walk.piles[2].cards} | <code>{v(walk.piles[2].vector.first)}</code> | {walk.piles[2].vector.norm} | {walk.piles[2].vector.zeros} |
| exhaust | {walk.piles[3].cards} | <code>{v(walk.piles[3].vector.first)}</code> | {walk.piles[3].vector.norm} | {walk.piles[3].vector.zeros} |

An empty pile is exactly the zero vector. These four join into the 256 numbers of the cards.

## 5. The powers

The player has one power, Noxious Fumes with amount {walk.state.powers[0].amount}, scaled to 0.3. Its vector is its id's vector plus 0.3 times its amount direction. One layer of 32 → 64 maps it, and with one power the sum is that one output: <code>{v(walk.powers.first)}</code>, with {walk.powers.zeros} of 64 at 0.

## 6. The enemies

Each enemy joins three parts, and the same $\varphi$ reads both:

| | first Scroll | second Scroll |
|---|---|---|

| powers' sum (64) | <code>{v(walk.enemies[0].powers_vector)}</code> | <code>{v(walk.enemies[1].powers_vector)}</code> |
| slot 0, of 4 slots (32 each) | <code>{v(walk.enemies[0].slot_vector)}</code> | <code>{v(walk.enemies[1].slot_vector)}</code> |
| after $\varphi$ (128): length | {walk.enemies[0].phi.norm} | {walk.enemies[1].phi.norm} |
| after $\varphi$: numbers at 0 | {walk.enemies[0].phi.zeros} | {walk.enemies[1].phi.zeros} |

The two enemies have one id, so they share entry {walk.enemies[0].entry}. They differ by their numbers, their powers' amounts and their intents, which is why their vectors differ.

The sum of the two is the enemies' part: 128 numbers of length {walk.foes.norm}, of which {walk.foes.zeros} are 0. Only numbers that are 0 in both stay 0.

## 9. The body

Two layers, and everything meets for the first time:

| layer | first numbers | length | numbers at 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 651 → 256 | <code>{v(walk.body[0].first)}</code> | {walk.body[0].norm} | {walk.body[0].zeros} |
| 256 → 256 | <code>{v(walk.body[1].first)}</code> | {walk.body[1].norm} | {walk.body[1].zeros} |

Most of the 256 numbers are 0 for this state; the {256 - walk.body[1].zeros} that are not carry everything the heads will read.

## 10. The endings head: 51 logits

One linear layer gives 51 raw numbers. The first is the death's, {walk.head.death_logit}. A logit has no meaning alone: only the softmax in the next step, which compares them, makes them chances.

## 11. The mask, then the softmax

The HP share is {walk.value.share}. A bucket $[l, l + 0.025)$ stays open if $l < 0.8873$ and $l + 0.025 > 0.8873 - 1$. Here:

- The bottom: a fight cannot heal more than the HP missing. {walk.state.max_hp - walk.state.hp} of {walk.state.max_hp} HP is a share of 0.113, so the buckets below {walk.head.open_from} are closed.
- The top: a win cannot lose all the HP held, so the buckets from {walk.head.open_to} up are closed.

That leaves **{walk.head.open} of the 50 buckets open**. The others get a logit of $-10^4$, and the softmax gives them exactly 0.

The softmax over the rest gives the chances. A death has {walk.head.death}, next to nothing. The most likely buckets:

| share of max HP still to be lost | in HP | chance |
|---|---|---|
| {walk.head.tops[0].low} to {walk.head.tops[0].high} | 0 to 1.8 | {pct(walk.head.tops[0].chance)} |
| {walk.head.tops[1].low} to {walk.head.tops[1].high} | a heal of up to 1.8 | {pct(walk.head.tops[1].chance)} |
| {walk.head.tops[2].low} to {walk.head.tops[2].high} | a heal of 1.8 to 3.6 | {pct(walk.head.tops[2].chance)} |
| {walk.head.tops[3].low} to {walk.head.tops[3].high} | 1.8 to 3.6 | {pct(walk.head.tops[3].chance)} |

The net is close to certain that this fight is won with at most a scratch. The second Scroll holds 16 poison at 19 HP, and the player's Noxious Fumes adds more each turn; the net read those numbers, though nothing here says which inputs moved it most.

## 12. The potion head

The same 256 numbers go through a second linear layer, of 256 → {walk.potion.outputs}, one output per entry of the potion table. Attack Potion is entry {walk.potion.entry}. Its logit is {walk.potion.logit}, and the sigmoid makes that **{walk.potion.chance}**: the net expects the potion to still be there after the fight.

The other {walk.potion.outputs - 1} outputs are computed and not read. The largest here are {walk.potion.most_likely[0].id} at {walk.potion.most_likely[0].chance} and `<unk>` at {walk.potion.most_likely[1].chance}: this state holds neither, so they mean nothing and train nothing.

## 13. The number the search reads

`CombatNet.Value` prices the 51 chances ([The leaf §5](/en/arch/combat-brain/leaf/#sec-5)):

$$
\text{value} = \sum_{k=1}^{50} p_k \big(0.2 + 0.6\,(s - m_k)\big) + (1 - p_0)\times 0.02 \sum_{\text{held}} (1 - u_{\text{id}})
$$

| term | number |
|---|---|
| the endings, summed over the open buckets | {walk.value.win_term} |
| potions expected to be kept, $\sum (1 - u)$ | {walk.value.kept} |
| their term, $(1 - p_0) \times 0.02 \times$ that | {walk.value.potion_term} |
| **the value** | **{walk.value.value}** |

The planner's **bound** for this state is a win with no HP or potion lost: 0.2 + 0.6 × {walk.value.share} + 0.02 = {walk.value.bound}. The value sits just below it, since the net expects almost nothing to be lost.

## 14. What a search sees

A search compares states. Change one thing at a time and run the net again:

| state | HP share | endings | potions | value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| as it is | {walk.value.share} | {walk.value.win_term} | {walk.value.potion_term} | {walk.value.value} |
| {walk.lost_hp} HP less | {walk.siblings.less_hp.share} | {walk.siblings.less_hp.win_term} | {walk.siblings.less_hp.potion_term} | {walk.siblings.less_hp.value} |
| the potion gone | {walk.siblings.no_potion.share} | {walk.siblings.no_potion.win_term} | {walk.siblings.no_potion.potion_term} | {walk.siblings.no_potion.value} |

{walk.lost_hp} HP is a share of 0.141 of 71, which at 0.6 is 0.0845, and the value falls by just that. The HP held counts in full, as it was built to ([The combat net §7](/en/arch/combat-brain/network/#sec-7)). In this state the potion is worth its flat price, 0.02, the same as 2.4 HP.

So when a beam compares two ends of the same turn, one that kept 10 more HP is ahead by that much before the net has formed any opinion on what comes next.

| HP | {walk.others.danger.hp} / {walk.others.danger.max_hp} | {walk.others.mask.hp} / {walk.others.mask.max_hp} |
| open buckets | {walk.others.danger.open} ({walk.others.danger.open_from} to {walk.others.danger.open_to}) | {walk.others.mask.open} ({walk.others.mask.open_from} to {walk.others.mask.open_to}) |
| chance of a death | {pct(walk.others.danger.death)} | {pct(walk.others.mask.death)} |
| the likeliest bucket | {walk.others.danger.tops[0].low} to {walk.others.danger.tops[0].high}, {pct(walk.others.danger.tops[0].chance)} | {walk.others.mask.tops[0].low} to {walk.others.mask.tops[0].high}, {pct(walk.others.mask.tops[0].chance)} |

In the boss fight the value is far below the bound: a chance of death takes that share of the value away, and the wins are spread over many buckets. At 10 HP only {walk.others.mask.open} buckets are open, so no share of the chances can fall on a win that loses more HP than the player holds.

## 16. The label

So far the net has predicted. In training this state also has an answer, from how its fight really ended. The file does not keep that ending, so take one for illustration: **suppose the fight is won at {walk.label.end_hp} of 71 HP, with the potion still held**.

- **The ending's class.** The share lost from this state is 63/71 − {walk.label.end_hp}/71 = {walk.label.lost}, in the bucket from {walk.label.low} to {walk.label.high}: class {walk.label.class}. Every round start of that fight would get its own class, from its own HP.
- **The potion's label.** It is still in the first run view after the fight, so its label is 0.
- **The loss.** The chance of class {walk.label.class} was {walk.label.chance}, so the endings' cross-entropy is {walk.label.ending_loss}. The potion's is {walk.label.potion_loss}. The sample's loss is their sum, and a gradient step would push that bucket's chance up.

A large loss like this one is what a surprise looks like: under this supposed ending, the net was badly wrong. How labels are made across a whole list of runs is in [Training the combat net §3](/en/arch/learning/combat-net/#sec-3).
