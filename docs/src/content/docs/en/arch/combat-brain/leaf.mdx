Every search stops somewhere. PIMC stops at the end of the turn; the SL planner usually reaches the end of the fight, but it too has to rank the states where its turns end before it knows how the fight goes on ([The SL planner: beam §1](/en/arch/sl-planner/beam/#sec-1)). The state where a search stops is a **leaf**, and the function that says what it is worth is the **leaf value**. In one line: c2's leaf is the net's distribution of endings times a price for each; c7-1v's is one learned share of V. This page explains the two leaves the brain has, `LeafValue`, written by hand, and the combat net c2, which the champions of both environments play ([§2](#sec-2)–[§6](#sec-6)); c2's known issues ([§7](#sec-7)); and SL's next net, c7-1v, built and in training ([§8](#sec-8)).

## 1. What a leaf is

A leaf is either a state at the start of a turn, after the enemies' turn has been played for real, or the end of the fight, won or lost. Both are valued by the same function, `ICombatLeaf.Value(status, observation, history)`: a lost fight is worth nothing, a won one by what it leaves, and a fight in progress by what it is expected to leave.

- **Observation only.** The leaf reads what the player sees, plus the public record of the enemies' moves so far ([Prompts, moves and workers §2](/en/arch/combat-brain/shared/#sec-2)). It never reads the draw order or a random stream, so it cannot leak hidden state, in either environment.
- **One scale.** Its unit is the run's: a share of a win, P(win run) as the design states it ([Macro brain: from hazards to value §1](/en/arch/macro-brain/value/#sec-1)). The weights below make it that only roughly; with V's prices it is V's unit ([§5](#sec-5)).
- **Ordering, more than level.** A search uses the leaf to compare lines: what matters is that a better state gets a higher value. A leaf that rates every state 0.1 too high plays the same as one that does not.

## 2. The hand-made leaf: LeafValue

`LeafValue` (v0, `src/Sts2Ai.Brain/Combat/LeafValue.cs`) projects the rest of the fight from what is on the screen, with a few numbers set by hand.

- A lost fight is 0. A won fight with HP share $s$ left (HP over max HP) is worth $\mathrm{Win}(s) = 0.2 + 0.6\,s$, plus 0.02 for each potion still held.
- A fight in progress is projected to its end. Each living enemy $i$ has an **effective HP** $e_i$ (its HP plus block, less 0.8 times the damage its poison will still deal, $n + (n-1) + \dots + 1$ for $n$ poison) and a **damage rate** $r_i$ (the attack it shows this turn, damage × hits, at least 3). The player deals $d = 10 + 2 \times \text{Strength}$ a turn and kills the enemies one after another.
- The order is the one that loses least HP. Enemy $i$ hits until it dies, at the turn $t_i = \sum_{j \preceq i} e_j / d$ (the sum over the enemies killed before it and itself), so the damage taken is $\sum_i r_i\, t_i$. **Smith's rule**, from scheduling, minimizes such a weighted sum of completion times: take the jobs in decreasing order of weight per length, here $r_i / e_i$. Half of the damage beyond the current block is taken off the HP, and the HP left is scored as a win:

$$
\text{value} = \mathrm{Win}\!\left(\frac{\text{HP} - 0.5\,\max\!\big(0,\ \sum_i r_i t_i - \text{block}\big)}{\text{max HP}}\right) + 0.02 \times \text{potions}
$$

- A projected death (a share below 0) decays toward 0 instead of going negative, $\mathrm{Win}(s) = 0.2\, e^{3 s}$ for $s < 0$, with the same slope at 0. It stays above a loss and keeps ordering lines however hopeless: with a flat floor every losing line looked the same, and the brain then ended its turns early.

So damage is worth more on an enemy that hits hard and dies soon, overkill is worth nothing, and a state in progress never beats the win it could reach with the same HP. That last property is what the SL planner's bound relies on ([The SL planner: beam §2](/en/arch/sl-planner/beam/#sec-2)).

Its blind spots are everything it does not read: the enemies' powers other than poison, the player's powers other than Strength, the draw pile, and every intent after this turn. The numbers (`LeafWeights`: WinBase 0.2, PerHp 0.6, PerPotion 0.02, Dps 10, Unblocked 0.5, MinIncoming 3, PoisonShare 0.8) are hypotheses, never tuned: `--leaf FILE` gives a run, a panel or the brain other weights (`weights --leaf` prints the defaults as such a file, `leaf0`), but no tool tunes them any more, since the strategy is left to learned values ([Learning: the loop §6](/en/arch/learning/loop/#sec-6)).

## 3. The combat net

The combat net (`CombatNet`, `--combat-net FILE`) replaces the projection with a prediction learned from fights that were played. It reads a round's start and predicts how the fight will end under the policy that played its training games.

**What it reads.** Everything about the fight that the observation shows, as tokens:

- every card in the four piles (hand, draw, discard, exhaust): its id, its upgrade level, its enchantment and affliction, and which pile it is in;
- the player: HP share, HP, max HP, block, energy, max energy, stars, the round, and its powers (id and amount);
- each living enemy: its id, HP share, HP, max HP, block, how many rounds it has shown, its powers, and its **intent slots**: the intents it shows now, then those of its last 3 rounds, most recent first;
- the relics, the potions, and the context (character, encounter, room, act).

Nothing about a monster's moves is written by hand. From the monster's id, the round and its last intents, the net can learn each monster's move order, and where in it the monster is, from data. An experimental next-intent head named the next round's intent type for 57.7% of the enemies, against 28.9% for repeating the current one, and most of its misses were moves the game rolls at random ([NoSL PIMC §1](/en/arch/pimc/#sec-1)).

**How it reads them.** A set encoder (a *Deep Set*, Zaheer et al., 2017: a function of the sum of its elements' vectors gives the same output for every order and reads sets of any size): each card is the sum of learned vectors (its id's, its upgrade level times its id's upgrade vector, its modifiers' and its pile's), a small MLP maps each card, and each pile is the sum of its cards (× 1/8). An enemy's slot is the sum of its intents (the type's vector plus damage / 10 and total damage / 20 times vectors of that type); an MLP maps each enemy with its powers and slots, and the enemies are summed. Relics, potions and the context are sums of their vectors. Two layers of 256 then give the head. A sum ignores order and reads any number of cards or enemies, which is what a pile and a fight need.

## 4. The head: how the fight ends

The head is a probability distribution over 51 classes: a **death**, or one of 50 buckets of the share of max HP **still to be lost** from this state to the end of a won fight, spanning −0.25 (a fight that heals) to 1. A second output gives the potions still to be used after a win.

Why "still to be lost" and not "left at the end"? A net that predicted the share left at the end learned only about half of what the HP held now is worth there: one HP less now lowered its value by 0.0038, where it lowers the end by a full HP, 0.0078 at `LeafValue`'s prices. Its search traded HP away too cheaply and failed its gate. Predicting what is still to come makes the HP held now count in full, since it is added back exactly when the value is formed (0.0083 per HP on the same states), and the error of the value fell from 0.0090 to 0.0077 ([Training the combat net §6](/en/arch/learning/combat-net/#sec-6)).

The two boss fights at the left are the hard ones: a death in 86% and 77% of the endings.

On its held-out runs c2's cross-entropy is 1.32 nats, against 2.00 for always predicting the training set's frequencies; it is lowest at bosses (1.13) and highest at elites (2.64), of which there were few. The value it implies correlates 0.959 with the real ending's value, `LeafValue`'s 0.790.

The figure also shows a flaw. A won fight cannot lose more HP than the player holds, so every bucket right of the dashed line is impossible for a win; nothing tells the head so. On the easy states it puts no weight there, but in the two boss fights it does: 83% of The Kin's win mass and 21% of Vantom's. At `LeafValue`'s prices, which the combat net applies linearly, those impossible wins count below a win at 0 HP (0.2), the deepest ones even below a death (0) ([§7](#sec-7)).

## 5. Pricing the endings

A distribution is not yet a number. The leaf turns it into one with a price for each ending. With `LeafValue`'s prices (the default), for HP share $s$ now, the probability $p_0$ of a death, $p_k$ of bucket $k$ with middle $m_k$, and $u$ potions used after a win out of $q$ held:

$$
\text{value} = \sum_{k=1}^{50} p_k \big(0.2 + 0.6\,(s - m_k)\big) + (1 - p_0)\times 0.02\,(q - u)
$$

A death costs nothing extra: it is worth 0. A fight that is already over is valued as `LeafValue` values it.

**V's prices** (`--leaf-prices v`, built, not in use). These are the prices of a net of target `worth`, the only kind of net they go with, and c2 is refused with them. Every state is in V's own unit (`PricedLeaf`, `Brain/Combat/PricedLeaf.cs:17-49`):

- a death is worth 0;
- a win is worth V of the run with the HP and the potions it ends with. For each subset of the potions held, V is asked at each HP from 0 to max, and taken as the most of it at that HP or less, so that a win with more HP is never worth less, which the planner's bound needs. A potion the fight gave has no price, as V was asked only of those held at the fight's start;
- a state in progress is worth the net's number, clamped between 0 and the file's `worth_cap`, times V of the run at the fight's first plan.

The prices are made once a fight, at its first plan, on the host, and sent to the planner's workers with each request (`EndPrices`), so the workers need no V. That is one table of V per distinct subset of the potions held. It needs `--policy plan`, a `--combat-net` of target `worth` and a net `--value`; NoSL's search keeps the leaf weights' prices.

**What these prices do not see.** A finished line sees gold and the deck as they were at the first plan; only the net, through its labels, learns what a fight takes from them. A win's price rises with HP by construction and with potions only as far as V says so: nothing forces a win that keeps a potion above one that does not. Labels and prices both belong to the V that made them, so a later V means labels computed again.

## 6. One net per environment

A net predicts the endings of the policy that played its games. SL's planner, which sees what NoSL's search must guess, wins more fights from the same state, so weights belong to one environment, while the structure is shared: a net file names its environment, and a run of the other refuses it. c1–c4 predate that field and name none.

That leaves an open seam: c2 learned from NoSL fights played by PIMC with `LeafValue`, and it is the leaf of the SL planner too. Its endings are those of a weaker player than the planner. No SL-trained net has ever been gated. <Badge text="in training" variant="caution" /> SL's own net is c7-1v, from SL self-play ([Training the combat net §7](/en/arch/learning/combat-net/#sec-7)); NoSL stays on c2 for now.

## 7. Known issues of c2

c2 was written by `train_combat.py` at commit `b4480a1`, removed since, on 315 NoSL runs. Read against whole SL runs of the champion (v7-1f, 100 runs on `promotion-1-100`, 60 deaths: 52 at bosses, 6 at elites, 2 in hallway fights), it has these gaps:

- **No act 3 in its vocabulary.** Its training runs reach acts 1 and 2 only, so none of Glory's 18 encounters has an id of its own, and 32 of the 60 deaths came in encounters outside its vocabulary. 46 of 121 monsters and 195 of 268 powers are missing too; not all of those can reach Silent, and how often they appear has not been measured.
- **Unknown powers collide.** Every power outside the vocabulary reads as the one unknown token, so Envenom, Burst, Tools of the Trade and Well-Laid Plans give identical outputs. On the Vantom test state that is worth 0.0083 of value, about 1 HP.
- **What an all-unknown fight still reads.** With the act, encounter and enemy all unknown, the HP and poison slopes keep their sign: 30 poison adds 0.167, against 0.278 when known.
- **Impossible buckets can value below a death.** Nothing masks the buckets beyond the HP held ([§4](#sec-4)).
- **It never saw the planner play.** Its labels are PIMC's endings ([§6](#sec-6)).
- **Potions at a flat price.** Its prices count potions at 0.02 each and gold and lost cards not at all, while what a potion is worth depends on the deck. The bots hold 0.1 potions per round start; whether the flat price causes that is not shown.

**On SL's round starts.** On v7g1's held-out runs c2's cross-entropy is 2.086 against a prior's 2.147, its mean chance of death 0.162 against an actual 0.055, its rank correlation within (encounter, round) 0.814. On medium-budget runs on `promotion-301-500` those are 3.264 against 2.119, 0.275 against 0.039, and 0.696; at their bosses 6.56 against 3.58, 0.803 against 0.184, and 0.470. At Test Subject the rank correlation is 0.016 held out and 0.206 at medium. On the medium runs it does not know 21.6 % of the enemies, 23.7 % of the relics, 28.9 % of the modifiers, 7.0 % of the powers and 3.6 % of the cards.

Not measured: how many deaths a bigger beam or another leaf would turn into wins.

## 8. The next net: c7-1v <Badge text="in training" variant="caution" />

`learn/combat/` and the prices of [§5](#sec-5) are built. c7-1v is being trained and has not been gated, so nothing here is a result. It is the only net of this step, and V is not trained for it: v7-1f computes the labels and the prices.

### 8.1 What it reads

The input rule: **the state of the fight is in the input; what follows from an id is the net's to learn.**

| input | read | what it adds or leaves out |
|---|---|---|
| cards | id, upgrade, pile, modifier ids and amounts, current cost | the current cost, which effects change and the id does not say, and each modifier's amount (`ModifierView.Amount`), which 11 of the 23 enchantments use, Sharp among them |
| player | HP share, HP, max HP, block, energy, max energy, stars, round | – |
| powers | id, amount, stacks, kept card | – |
| enemies | id, HP share, HP, max HP, block, rounds shown, powers, intents now and in the last 3 rounds | – |
| relics | id, counter, melted, status | the counter and the melted flag, and the status, which 42 relics set; the id says none of them. The status is its own input and no suffix on the id |
| potions, orbs, allies | their ids; an ally's numbers and powers | – |
| context | the encounter | act, room and character are still fed, but the vocabulary lists only encounters, so they read as `<unk>`: the encounter implies them |
| 3 numbers | the ascension, orb slots, gold | – |

**Not read:** other printed numbers, which follow from the id and the upgrade; the draw order, since the net reads what a player sees (SL's ordered draw is a separate, later experiment).

The added numbers go in through the graph inputs `costs`, `mod_amounts`, `relic_values` and `relic_status`, which `NetNumbers` already feeds. A card's `Vars` stay out for Silent: only Claw, Rampage, Maul, Thrash, KinglyPunch, SovereignBlade and TheBall change their own numbers in a fight, and none of them is in Silent's pool.

**A limit of the observation.** Per-combat relic flags, Centennial Puzzle's for one, are not in the observation at all, so no input rule can reach them.

The vocabulary is built from its training runs, which reach act 3. There is no id dropout: this vocabulary leaves 0 unknown ids on the medium test set.

### 8.2 Its structure and its output

The diagram draws the graph `learn/combat/net.py` builds; no trained file of it has been gated.

<ArchDiagram client:visible lang="en" net="combatNext" />

Embeddings of 32. A card goes through an MLP of 32 → 64 → 64, summed per pile × 1/8: 256 numbers in all. The player is 8 numbers. Powers go through an MLP of 32 → 64 and are summed: 64. An enemy is 224 numbers (32 + 64 + 4 × 32) through an MLP of 224 → 128 → 128, summed: 128. Relics, potions, the context and orbs are 32 each, allies 64, and 3 extra numbers. A body of 651 → 256 → 256 reads them, at width 1.

The head is one number, the fight's **worth**, with no buckets, no potion head and no policy head:

```
worth = min(4, V(first run_view after the fight) / V(last run_view before it))
a death: 0
a fight that wins the run: V after it = 1
```

V reads the deck, the relics, the potions, the HP and the gold, so what a fight spends is priced by what it means for this run, and what a potion is worth depends on the deck as it does for V. Its samples and loss are in [Training the combat net §3](/en/arch/learning/combat-net/#sec-3)–[§4](/en/arch/learning/combat-net/#sec-4).

### 8.3 The net in a search

The net reads one state at a time. `TurnEnds` lists a turn's ends, and the leaf values each: the search and the net meet only there ([Combat brain §3](/en/arch/combat-brain/#sec-3)). The search ranks by the net's share times V before the fight, and ends a line at V's price of its end ([§5](#sec-5)). A number above 1 is a state the net expects the fight to improve: a fight's reward can raise V.

### 8.4 How it is judged

The report gives the squared error and the rank correlation within (encounter, round) groups, on the whole and by act, room and encounter, since the beam ranks states of one fight. These inform; the paired gate decides.

**The gate.** `--combat-net weights/silent/c7-1v.json` with the SL champion's other options, on the node's clone, no exploration: 300 runs at medium and 300 at the low budget on `promotion-1-300`, seeds it never trained on. It passes when `wins_lower_95` is above 0 at medium; a pair's standard error is about 3.15 points on 300, so that takes about +5.2 points, +16 wins. The low budget decides nothing. Test Subject decides nothing either: its 16 wins in v7g1 may be too few to learn from, and most deaths come before its third form (120 / 183 / 28 by form).

One gate holds three changes, the labels, the inputs and the prices, so a failure cannot be split between the net and V's prices.

| result | reading |
|---|---|
| better rank, passes | the leaf was a bottleneck; priced endings work |
| better rank, wins and floor flat | at this budget the leaf is not the bottleneck: the search or V. Not settled for the hardest fights, given their few won labels |
| low improves, medium flat | the leaf matters only in a narrow beam |
| worse | read HP lost per won fight, potions used and elites first |
