Every search stops somewhere. PIMC stops at the end of the turn; the SL planner usually reaches the end of the fight, but it too has to rank the states where its turns end before it knows how the fight goes on ([The SL planner: beam §1](/en/arch/sl-planner/beam/#sec-1)). The state where a search stops is a **leaf**, and the function that says what it is worth is the **leaf value**. In one line: the leaf is the net's distribution of endings, priced ending by ending, plus a small price for the potions it expects to be kept.

## 1. What a leaf is

A leaf is either a state at the start of a turn, after the enemies' turn has been played for real, or the end of the fight, won or lost. Both are valued by the same function, `ICombatLeaf.Value(status, observation, history)`: a lost fight is worth nothing, a won one by what it leaves, and a fight in progress by what it is expected to leave.

- **Observation only.** The leaf reads what the player sees, plus the public record of the enemies' moves so far ([Prompts, moves and workers §2](/en/arch/combat-brain/shared/#sec-2)). It never reads the draw order or a random stream, so it cannot leak hidden state, in either environment.
- **Ordering, more than level.** A search uses the leaf to compare lines: what matters is that a better state gets a higher value. A leaf that rates every state 0.1 too high plays the same as one that does not.

## 2. The prices of an ending

Prices are numbers set by hand in `LeafValue` (`LeafValue.Of`, `src/Sts2Ai.Brain/Combat/LeafValue.cs:50-77`):

- A lost fight is 0.
- A won fight with HP share $s$ left (HP over max HP) and $q$ potions held is worth

$$
\text{Win}(s, q) = 0.2 + 0.6\,s + 0.02\,q.
$$

They are the only hand-set numbers in the leaf with a net. A fight that is already over is valued by them directly, with no net: the end of every finished line, and so every plan's final score, is this formula on the state the line ends in.

With no `--combat-net`, a fight in progress goes to `LeafValue` too (`HandLeaf`, `src/Sts2Ai.SimWorker/Brains/LeafChoice.cs:20`): it projects the rest of the fight from what is on the screen, with a few more numbers set by hand. No champion plays that way.

## 3. The combat net

For a fight still in progress the leaf asks the combat net. It reads a round's start and predicts how the fight will end under the policy that played its training games. Its inputs and layers are in [The combat net](/en/arch/combat-brain/network/), a real state followed through it is in [One state through the net](/en/arch/combat-brain/walkthrough/), and its training is in [Training the combat net](/en/arch/learning/combat-net/).

`--combat-net FILE` plays it, in the planner (shown as `plan NAME`) or in PIMC (`pimc NAME`).

## 4. The two outputs

**The endings.** 51 chances: a death, or one of 50 buckets of the share of max HP still to be lost from this state to the end of a won fight, from −0.25 (a fight that heals) to 1. The buckets the HP held rules out are closed, with a chance of exactly 0 ([The combat net §7](/en/arch/combat-brain/network/#sec-7)).

**The potions.** After a win, each held potion's chance of being gone.

The figure shows them on the 16 round starts the net file carries, real states from held-out runs; loading the net checks that it gives these outputs, as the trainer computed them. The shaded bands are the closed buckets. Weights: `weights/silent/c7-2.json`.

## 5. Pricing the endings

A distribution is not yet a number. The leaf gives each ending its price from [§2](#sec-2). For HP share $s$ now, the chance $p_0$ of a death, $p_k$ of bucket $k$ with middle $m_k$, and the chance $u_{\text{id}}$ that a held potion is gone after a win:

$$
\text{value} = \sum_{k=1}^{50} p_k \big(0.2 + 0.6\,(s - m_k)\big) + (1 - p_0)\times 0.02 \sum_{\text{held}} (1 - u_{\text{id}})
$$

A death adds nothing, since it is worth 0 (`CombatNet.Value`, `src/Sts2Ai.Brain/Combat/CombatNet.cs:74-86`). Term by term, on a real state, in [One state through the net §13](/en/arch/combat-brain/walkthrough/#sec-13).

The form is **what the state holds, exactly, plus what the net says is still to come**. $s$ enters exactly, so states of one beam differ by their true HP before the net says anything. A potion drunk leaves the sum exactly.

**The bound.** The planner prunes by the value of a win at the state's HP and potions, which is $\text{Win}(s, q)$ of [§2](#sec-2) and no net ([The SL planner: beam §2](/en/arch/sl-planner/beam/#sec-2)). A bucket's middle may pass the HP held by half a width, and a net can predict healing, so a state in progress can rise above its bound by a small amount: 1.84 % of the won round starts in the training data healed at all, and at full HP the mask forbids it.

**Where the net acts.** Pruning and the choice among finished lines read the prices alone. The net decides the beam's order, what the beam keeps, the tie among losing lines, and a line stopped at the turn cap. In PIMC, it values every leaf at the end of the turn.

## 6. One net, two environments

SL and NoSL play the same net file. It learned from SL's games, so its endings are those of the planner, which sees what NoSL's search must guess and wins more from the same state. In NoSL the same state is therefore rated by a stronger player's outcomes.

## 7. What this value cannot see

These follow from the prices and the labels.

- **A flat price per potion.** A potion is worth 0.02 whichever it is and whichever fight it is, as much as 2.3 HP at 70 max HP. So no head can make the planner save a potion for a boss; only other prices could. The potion head learns when potions go, and the value reads only the sum.
- **Gold.** It is an input, but a fight's change of gold has no price, here or on a finished line. Thievery and Seal of Gold cost nothing.
- **Max HP.** It enters only through the share: 70/70 to 66/66 reads as nothing lost, so Paper Cuts and Brightest Flame are free, on finished lines too.
- **The rest of the run.** A finished line sees only HP and potions. What a fight's deck, relics or rewards mean for the run is outside the leaf.
- **Siblings.** The logs hold one ending per fight, so no label separates two states a search compares within one round. What separates them is HP held, counted in full, and what the net learned across fights.
- **Its own player.** The endings are those of the policy that played the training games, mostly at a cheaper budget than the one that plays gates ([Training the combat net §3](/en/arch/learning/combat-net/#sec-3)).
